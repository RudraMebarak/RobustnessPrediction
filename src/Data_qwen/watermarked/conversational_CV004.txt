If one friend is currently in a bad mood and the other is in a good mood, calculate the expected value for the total distance traveled by all three friends if the cinema distance is twice the home distance.

To determine the expected value for the total distance traveled by all three friends, we need to consider the possible outcomes of their decisions and the associated probabilities. Let's break it down step by step.

### Step 1: Determine the distances
Let \( D_H \) be the distance from your home to the cinema, which is given as the cinema distance.
The distance from your home to your friends' homes are:
\[ d_1 = D_H + d_{1H} \]
\[ d_2 = D_H + d_{2H} \]

### Step 2: Calculate the distance for each friend's movement
For your friends:
- Moving from your home to your friends’ homes takes distance \( d_1 \) if you choose the home version or \( d_2 \) if you choose the cinema.
- Moving from your friends’ homes back to your home takes distance \( d_1 \) if you choose the home version or \( d_2 \) if you choose the cinema.
- Moving from your friends’ homes back to your friends’ homes takes distance \( d_1 \) if you choose the home version or \( d_2 \) if you choose the cinema.
- Moving from your friends’ homes back to your friends’ homes takes distance \( d_1 \) if you choose the home version or \( d_2 \) if you choose the cinema.

### Step 3: Probability analysis
Each friend has an equal probability of either choosing the home version or going to the cinema. Therefore, the probability that any friend chooses the home version is:
\[ P(\text{choose home}) = P(\text{choose cinema}) = 0.5 \]

### Step 4: Expected values
We need to compute the expected value for the total distance traveled by all three friends for the cinema distance case (\( d_1 \)) and the home distance case (\( d_2 \)).

#### Case 1: Cinema distance
For every move made (home to friends’ home, friends’ home to friends’ home, friends’ home to friends’ home), there are two choices:
1. Home -> Friends' home -> Friends' home -> Friends' home -> Home

The probability of taking these moves alternately follows binomial trials with respect to flipping a fair coin (heads → home, tails → cinema).
