Although GDP is the most widely used metric to measure a country's standard of living or development, it is not entirely reliable due to several constraints. 1. Single Focus: GDP measures only monetary transactions and does not account for non-market activities such as unpaid household labor or cultural activities. 2. Focusing too much on short-term issues: By using GDP statistics, policymakers can only allocate resources to industries within their jurisdiction that contribute to sustainable development. 3. Concentrate on environmental factors To achieve sustainable and inclusive economic development, it is essential to use comprehensive and disaggregated statistics to inform policy responses that reflect the actual needs and aspirations of citizens across different socioeconomic levels. However, many countries rely on single-minded GDP growth targets determined by financial markets, leading to a dearth of essential indicators. The Social Protection Index (ISP) tracks the effectiveness and coverage of social assistance and welfare programs, while the Regional Indicators compare regional differences in infrastructure, education, and health.
