The carbon cycle refers to the movement of carbon dioxide (CO2) across different time scales, including minutes, millions, and years. It can be traced back to volcanic activity, respiration, decomposition, volcanism, or other natural processes. Understanding the impact of CO2 on this cycle is crucial for predicting future climate change and how it may affect human societies. The combination of these elements can aid in comprehending the complexity and dynamics of the carbon cycle, as anthropogenic activities such as industrial processes, transportation, waste management, and power generation contribute to CO2 increase, which also leads to surface warming (IPCC 2021) and droughts in various regions. "Human metabolism as a sink for dissolved CO2" is the thesis of Smith, A. C. Thomas, M. W. Deamer, D." Rood, and E. F. Smith in 1995. Science 268(5190): 112.
