Policies aimed at improving the economic situation for low-income individuals, communities, and regions with higher needs can play a crucial role in mitigating socio-economic inequalities. The pursuit of these solutions necessitates collaboration among various actors, including federal, state/local governments, business leaders, civil society groups, academics, activists, professionals, and philanthropic institutions, to ensure that all individuals experience greater levels of equity and fairness, regardless of their background or location. These actions must continue until all citizens have access to basic necessities, financial stability, good schools, supportive family structures, safe places to work, undisturbed labor laws, unwavering commitment to justice, genuine human rights, ongoing efforts to achieve just results. Providing practical relief allows recipients to meet their immediate survival needs, which reduces the impact of financial instability. Social security systems can also act as buffers for anticipated and unexpected economic changes, such as layoffs and medical expenses. When properly structured, these safety nets can protect low-wage workers and retirees from significant drops in disposable income when unexpected burdens occur (OECD 2019). Progressive taxation is a fundamental strategy for mitigating socio-economic disparities. As household income increases, the tax brackets decrease, leading to reductions associated with this approach.
