The study aims to investigate the impact of technology access on student learning outcomes and academic achievement in rural educational institutions. Data collection will include standardized tests administered before and after the implementation of new tech-enabled programs (e.g., PARCC or Smarter Balanced assessments), as well as interviews with teachers, administrators, parents, and other students to gather information on perceived benefits, challenges encountered, attitudes towards technology adoption, pre- and post-assessment tools designed to measure teacher satisfaction, lack of funds and technical expertise among educators and administrative staff, inadequate bandwidth limitations, a willingness to invest in expensive devices and internet subscriptions. By identifying the significant factors contributing to the digital divide in our nation's most isolated communities, we aim to pave the way for more effective educational reforms aimed at closing the achievement gap. This study provides valuable insights that can be used by policy makers and educators to build a future where every child is equipped with the tools they need to thrive in the fast-paced global world, including those related to rural education, across various geographic areas of the US, as well as among other countries.
