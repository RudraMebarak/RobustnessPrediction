The development of antibiotic resistance in bacteria can be attributed to various mechanisms, such as target modification, induced mutations, and invasive drug production. The acquisition of antibiotic resistance by bacterial populations is influenced by several molecular mechanisms. The process of transposition involves the movement of a transposable element along the bacterial chromosomal sequence, integrating another copy of the resistance gene in close proximity. Resistance genes are then introduced into populations already harboring other bacteria, and they undergo natural selection and survival advantage due to antibiotic use.
