Quantum computing employs various techniques, including superposition, entanglement, interference, and teleportation, to carry out operations on data. The security risks associated with quantum computing are not limited to breaking existing encryption schemes, as it necessitates careful development strategies to balance potential vulnerabilities with practical benefits. This review highlights the importance of improving physical implementations and software architectures for achieving high-speed computational speed. Despite the excitement surrounding quantum computing advancements from IBM, Google, D-Wave, and Honeywell, widespread commercial adoption necessitates significant improvements in system reliability versus cost-effectiveness ratio to enable quantum computers to become mainstream tools in industry manufacturing processes and everyday consumer electronics products over time. To accelerate the transition from prototypical devices to practicalizable quantum processors, ongoing collaboration among academia and government funding bodies is necessary.
