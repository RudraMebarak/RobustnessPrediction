Antibiotic resistance can be caused by a variety of mechanisms, such as target modification, resulting in bacterial resistance. The molecular basis for antibiotic resistance is established by the moles found on plasmids, which can be transferred to other organisms. In bacterial populations, the movement of a transposable element along the chromosomal sequence is called transposition, which in turn allows for natural selection through the selective pressures that come with antibiotic use. Resistance genes that are introduced into other genomes will likely only persist in this population due to their fitness and survival advantages. Plasmids contain large genomic regions that enable multiple resistance genes, known as "genetic islands," which facilitates antibiotic resistance transmission across whole organisms.
