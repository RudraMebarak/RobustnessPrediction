Quantum Computing can be classified as superposition, entanglement, interference, teleportation, and quantum parallelism. It can also offer applications such as fast learning, faster communication, and enhanced efficiency in solving complex mathematical problems. Quantum computing presents a security threat not limited to breaking existing encryption schemes, but also requiring developing alternatives that are more resilient. This requires careful research and strategies to overcome challenges associated with practical implementation issues, including improving software architecture and building skills. Despite the increasing excitement surrounding quantum computing breakthroughs, commercial adoption requires significant improvements in system reliability versus cost-effectiveness ratio for quantum computers to become mainstream tools used in industry manufacturing processes and everyday consumer electronics products. To achieve this, we need to work together with academia and government funding bodies to accelerate innovation from prototypical devices to practicalizable quantum processors operating reliably under real world conditions for daily use within a reasonable timeframe of 2028-2030.
