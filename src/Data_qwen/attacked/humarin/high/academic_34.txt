Different applications such as virtual machines and distributed systems are commonly used in everyday cloud computing environments, but with additional considerations towards security and reliability. By utilizing microservices architecture, an application can be broken down into smaller components that can operate on its own in dedicated servers and scale according to different load points. Developers can also use containerization tools to package applications and their dependencies into portable bundles that can be deployed across various operating systems and clouds, which increases the ease of managing large-scale performance issues. Additionally, distributed systems offer superior flexibility, as they reduce latency and computational overhead, making it easier for organizations to solve complex business challenges in vertical expansion and lower dependence on central server resources. Constant energy requirements make vastly larger distributed infrastructures a concern, including environmental risks and operational expenses, but advancements in programming languages, frameworks, and architectures have made them more efficient, resilient, and accessible. Future research could focus on strategies for securely managing workloads across multiple layers of clouds, automating resource allocation over long periods, and contributing to unprecedented flexibility and scalability benefits beyond traditional approaches.
