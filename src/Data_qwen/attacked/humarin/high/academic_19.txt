The significance of studying the impact of technology on student learning outcomes and academic performance in rural educational institutions lies with the increased access to modern education. This study will examine the relationship between low-tech devices, internet accessibility, and academic achievement gaps in traditional classrooms. Specifically, they will analyze the effects of financial constraints and infrastructure limitations on students' motivation and learning. During the analysis process, qualitative data will be collected from standardized tests administered before and after new tech-enabled programs are introduced. It also includes interviews with teachers, administrators, parents, and students to determine the perceived benefits, challenges encountered, and attitudes towards technology adoption. By identifying the critical factors that contribute to the digital divide in our country's most isolated neighborhoods, we aim to pave the way for more effective educational reforms aimed at closing this achievement gap. The findings are anticipated to provide valuable lessons for educators and policymakers alike, as every rural child has access to the tools they need to thrive in today's fast-paced global society, regardless of their location or affiliation. Nonetheless, this research design can be used with other rural educational institutions across different geographic areas within the US or other countries experiencing comparable issues.
