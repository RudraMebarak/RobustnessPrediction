Quantum computing employs quantum mechanical phenomena such as superposition, entanglement, interference, teleportation, and quantum parallelism to carry out operations on data. The qubit is the fundamental unit of quantum information, allowing for faster communication between quantum systems and other entities. The security risks associated with quantum computing are not limited to breaking existing encryption schemes, as it necessitates careful development strategies to balance potential vulnerabilities with practical benefits. This review highlights the importance of improving physical implementations and software architectures for achieving high-speed computational speed. Despite the excitement surrounding the near-term success of quantum computers, commercial adoption necessitates significant improvements in system reliability compared to cost-effectiveness ratio before quantum computers can become mainstream tools in industry manufacturing processes and everyday consumer electronics products. To achieve this, we need to continue global collaboration among academia and government funding bodies.
