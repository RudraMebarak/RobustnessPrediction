International climate agreements have been instrumental in guiding global efforts to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, with their impacts on individual countries' emissions levels, global temperatures, economic development, social justice, and governance challenges. There are significant gaps in international climate agreements, as many countries are unable to meet their commitments under international frameworks. Natural Variability** is argued to be a contributing factor to CO2 increases, rather than human-caused warming, according to proponents. They also point out that natural factors are responsible for the majority of temperature changes over the past century, and suggest that there is no consistent anthropogenic impact since the late 18th century. However, critics argue that the increase in CO2 concentrations parallels improvements in resource efficiency and reduced dependence on fossil fuels, while examples of poor resource allocation and excessive use continue to drive higher emissions.
