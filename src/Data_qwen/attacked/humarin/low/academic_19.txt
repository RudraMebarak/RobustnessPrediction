The study aims to investigate the impact of technology access on student learning outcomes and academic achievement in rural educational institutions. Data collection will be conducted through standardized tests administered before and after the implementation of new tech-enabled programs (e.g., PARCC or Smarter Balanced assessments). Qualitative data will be collected through interviews with teachers, administrators, and students to determine the benefits, challenges encountered, and attitudes towards technology adoption. Pre- and post-assessment instruments will also be used to assess teacher satisfaction levels, insufficient bandwidth limitations, and parents' reluctance to invest in expensive devices and internet subscriptions. By identifying the key drivers of the digital divide in our nation's most isolated populations, we aim to pave the way for more effective educational reforms aimed at closing the achievement gap. This study's findings will have a significant positive impact on policy makers and educators, who hope that every rural child will have access to the tools and knowledge they need to thrive in a fast-paced global world, and that this research design could be applied to a range of rural schools in different geographic regions within the US or other countries with comparable issues.
