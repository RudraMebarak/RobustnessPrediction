The development of antibiotic resistance can be attributed to various mechanisms, such as target modification, Efflux pump production, and rapid dissemination of antibiotics across different populations. The acquisition of antibiotic resistance by bacterial populations is influenced by various molecular mechanisms, including plasmids, worms, and proteins. The process of transposition involves the movement of a transposable element along the bacterial chromosomal sequence, integrating another copy of the resistance gene nearby. Resistance genes are introduced into populations already harboring other bacterial genomes and undergo natural selection and survival advantage due to selective pressures imposed by antibiotic use.
