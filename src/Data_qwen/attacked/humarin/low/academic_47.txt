Although GDP is the most widely used indicator of a country's standard of living or development, it is not entirely reliable due to several constraints. 1. Single Focus: GDP measures only monetary transactions and does not account for non-market activities such as unpaid household labor or socially essential cultural activities. 2. Focusing too much on short-term GDP means policymakers cannot accurately predict the long-term sustainability of resources and can inadvertently allocate resources to these areas. 3. To achieve sustainable and inclusive economic development, it is essential to use comprehensive and disaggregated statistics to create effective policy responses that address the real needs and aspirations of citizens across different socioeconomic backgrounds. However, many countries rely on single-minded GDP growth targets established by financial markets, leading to a dearth of essential indicators.
