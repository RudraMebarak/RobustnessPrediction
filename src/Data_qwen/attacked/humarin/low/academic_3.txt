Examine the ways in which disparities in educational opportunities can perpetuate poverty and constrain social mobility, and identify potential solutions that could help alleviate these effects. Economic inequality and access to quality education are interdependent and have lasting consequences for individuals, families, communities, and societies as a whole. These disparities can be traced back to institutional biases that have played a significant role in shaping student success across different levels of society. Inadequate literacy and numeracy skills hinder employment opportunities, make individuals less vulnerable to exploitation, and hinder civic engagement, leading to a vicious cycle of underemployment and stagnant wages. To combat this, policymakers must work together on several reforms that prioritize equitable allocation of public funds for K-12 education, including a national lottery or block grants tied to neighborhood demographics (Kagan & Staiger, 1999), parental advisory councils, and professional development programs for diverse student populations, as well as ongoing workshops for learning innovative teaching methods.
