The creation of photovoltaic cells involves the use of semiconductor materials, particularly silicon, to create p-type and n-type semiconductors. When light hits the cell, electrons move freely as if they were ions. The direction of the electric current generated is dependent on the temperature of the semiconductor, which also affects the efficiency of the cell. The selection of materials is based on their ability to absorb different wavelengths of light and their compatibility with manufacturing processes, and they can be combined in multi-junction solar cells to achieve higher efficiencies. Additionally, modern research is exploring new materials such as Perovskite and Graphene-based cells, which may offer advantages such as reduced costs and improved thermal conductivity. Despite these advantages, polycrystalline silicon wafers may not offer the same level of purity or efficiency as monocrystalline silicon, which has lower relative values.
