Social media has both positive and negative impacts on the maintenance of friendships, and its effects are subjective based on individual experiences.

On one hand, social media can help people stay connected with friends who live far away by sharing photos, updates, and stories. This can strengthen relationships and make them feel more close-knit. Additionally, platforms like Facebook allow users to join groups focused on common interests or hobbies which can provide opportunities for virtual hangouts and discussions.

However, some argue that excessive use of social media can be isolating because frequent checking in can lead to missing out on real-life interactions with friends in person. Furthermore, the curated nature of many online posts might create unrealistic expectations about what friendships should look like, leading to disappointment when actual friendship dynamics do not match these ideals.

Moreover, there is evidence suggesting that social media may contribute to superficial friendships rather than deep connections that last over time. When individuals prioritize likes, comments, and followers over meaningful conversation and shared experiences, they tend to form less authentic bonds.

In conclusion, while social media can enhance connectivity and offer new ways to connect with friends across distances, it also presents challenges such as isolation if not used judiciously and potential risks regarding friend authenticity. Ultimately, balancing how often and how deeply one uses social media can affect how well friendships thrive long-term. 

To illustrate this balance, let's consider two extremes:
1. **Extremely Active:** Someone who regularly shares every detail of their life and rarely checks into reality.
   - **Impact**: Heightened connection but risk of superficial friendships and missed personal moments.
   
2. **Moderate Use:** Occasional updates supplemented by offline socializing.
   - **Impact**: Better retention of authentic connections and richer exchanges despite infrequent check-ins.

The key lies in striking an appropriate equilibrium between active engagement via social media and maintaining quality face-to-face relationships.
