**Critical Evaluation: The Prospects for AI-Driven Job Displacement**

The assertion that artificial intelligence (AI) is set to revolutionize economic landscapes by replacing significant proportions of human cerebral work holds considerable promise but also presents formidable challenges.

### Potential Benefits

1. **Productivity Enhancement:** AI can automate repetitive tasks at scale, potentially increasing productiveness and efficiency across various sectors (e.g., manufacturing, customer service). This could lead to cost savings and improved services for consumers and businesses alike.

2. **Skill Shifts:** As AI takes over routine jobs, there may be an increase in demand for skills interrelated to maintenance, management, and innovation. This could necessitate retraining efforts and may spur new career opportunities in fields like data science and robotics.

3. **Environmental Sustainability:** Automation could help reduce waste and pollution by optimizing procedures more efficiently. For instance, predictive analytics can mitigate energy consumption and expelling through smarter resource apportionment and production strategies.

4. **Healthcare Innovations:** AI-powered nosologies, virtual health assistants, and personalized treatments are already making waves in healthcare. These advancements could meliorate patient outcomes and extend life expectancy, benefiting global populations.

### Drawbacks and Ethical Concerns

1. **Job Loss and Economic Disruption:** While automation might initially increase productivity, it could paradoxically leave to job displacement if workers fail to adapt or are not adequately trained to handle the new technological landscape. This could exacerbate existing inequalities if unskilled workers face greater unemployment rates compared to skilled ones.

2. **Economic Inequality and Fairness:** Widespread AI adoption could widen income disparities as those who gain access to advanced skills lag behind those whose careers are rendered obsolete by automation.

3. **Ethical Considerations:** There are profound moral questions about who should bear the costs of implementing AI systems—those currently employed or society as a whole? If companies decide to invest AI technologies primarily in profitable endeavors rather than low-wage industries where humans are most affected, social welfare could suffer.

4. **Privacy Risks:** Increasingly sophisticated AI applications require vast amounts of personal data. The risk of AI being used for surveillance, bias against marginalised groups, or unauthorized use by corporations raises serious privacy and security concerns.

5. **Cultural Impact on Identity and Work Culture:** Changes brought about by automation might alter traditional notions of what constitutes "work." Workers could sense alienated from their roles, interrogating the very purpose of their existence due to shifts in the economy driven by AI.

### Mitigation Strategies

1. **Public Policy Support:** Governments must intervene to protect vulnerable workers through programs such as guaranteed minimal incomes or universal basic pensions to cushion them against job loss.

2. **Education and Training Programs:** Invest heavily in lifelong learning initiatives aimed at equipping people with the necessary technical skills demanded by emerging industries.

3. **Robust Labor Laws and Standards:** Strengthen regulations to ensure mediocre working conditions disregardless of whether a job takes manual labor or cognitive tasks.

4. **Rigorous Data Protection Measures:** Implement strong laws governing data collection and usage while safeguarding individuals' autonomy and privacy rights.

5. **Fair Share Models:** Advocate for equitable sharing models that benefit all members of society, considering everyone's contributions when planning economic policies.

### Conclusion

The prediction of a substantial replacement of human intellectual work by AI technology does hold significant potential benefits, peculiarly in terms of increased productivity and environmental sustainability. However, it poses substantial risks and ethical quandaries that must be addressed thoughtfully:

- Ensuring that the gains from AI are distributed fairly among order
- Providing enough support and training for displaced workers
- Balancing innovative technological advancement with robust privacy protections
- Addressing ethnical impacts on identity and work culture

Finally, navigating these complex challenges requires comprehensive policy frameworks contrived to protect citizens from the downsides of AI-driven transformation while harnessing its transformative power to uplift humanity as a whole. The path ahead promises both exciting possibilities and deep-seated challenges; thus far, only time will tell how we collectively choose to shape our future.
