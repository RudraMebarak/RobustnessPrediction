Social media has both positivist and negative impacts on the maintenance of friendships, and its effects are subjective grounded on individual experiences.

Additionally, platforms like Facebook allow users to join groups focused on common interests or hobbies which can provide opportunities for virtual hangouts and discussions.

However, some argue that excessive use of social media can be isolating because frequent checking in can lead to escaping out on real-life interactions with friends in person. Furthermore, the curated nature of many online posts might create unrealistic expectations about what friendships should look like, leading to disappointment when actual friendship dynamics don't match these ideals.

Moreover, there's evidence intimating that social media may contribute to superficial friendships rather than deep connections that last over time. When individuals prioritise likes, comments, and followers over meaningful conversation and shared experiences, they tend to form less authentic bonds.

In conclusion, while social media can enhance connectivity and offer new ways to connect with friends across distances, it likewise presents challenges such as isolation if not used judiciously and potential risks regarding friend authenticity.
