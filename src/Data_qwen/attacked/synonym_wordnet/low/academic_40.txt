Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a branch of artificial intelligence that deals with how agents should take actions in an environment to maximize some feeling of cumulative reward [1]. This approach has been successfully applied across various domains such as robotics, games, finance, transportation, etc.

The core idea behind RL is that an agent learns by interacting with its environment through repeated trials or observations of rewards and punishments (rewards are positive feedbacks for good behavior while punishments are negative feedbacks for bad behavior). The agent then uses this experience to update its policies - rules it follows to urinate decisions based on current observation - to improve future performance [2].

One fundamental assumption in RL is the concept of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), which assumes a state-space where transitions between states are probabilistic [3]. Each state corresponds to a particular situation or context, and each action corresponds to a possible choice of process within that situation. From any broken state, there exists several possible next commonwealths resulting from taking unlike activenesses, along with probabilities associated with these outcomes.

In essence, an RL agent starts in a particular state and hires one or more actions until reaching another state. In the new state, there may be a set of achievable states, and the agent can repeat the process by selecting among them again. 

A key feature of MDPs is the idea of "insurance", a function that maps states to activities, guiding the broker's decision-making process at every step. An optimum policy maximizes the expected cumulative payoff over all states, following a sequence of states and actions that leads to the highest overall satisfaction. Reinforcement Learning seeks to find such a policy by exploring the environment repeatedly and learning about what demeanours lead to high-pitched scores (positive rewards).
