Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is often cited as the most widely used metric to assess a country's overall standard of living or level of development. However, it has several limitations that make it an unreliable indicator:

1. **Single Focus**: GDP only measures monetary transactions and ignores non-market activities such as unpaid household labor, volunteer work, or cultural activities that are vital for human well-being. This means that many aspects of societal progress, including environmental sustainability, health care improvements, educational advancements, and social welfare programs are not reflected in GDP growth figures.

2. **Short-term Focus**: The concept of "GDP per capita" allows policymakers to focus solely on income levels within their jurisdictions rather than considering the long-term sustainability of resources and their equitable distribution among all members of society. A rising GDP does not necessarily equate to higher quality of life unless other factors like education, healthcare, and job security also improve over time.

3. **Resource Misallocation**: By focusing on economic indicators alone, governments may inadvertently allocate more capital and resources towards industries contributing less to overall welfare but which bring greater short-term benefits, leading to misallocations of scarce resources and potential waste.

4. **Environmental Degradation**: Rapid increases in industrial production can lead to pollution, habitat destruction, climate change, and degradation of ecosystems, affecting future generations' health and well-being without being reflected in current GDP figures.

These limitations highlight why GDP should be viewed primarily as an incomplete indicator. Certainly! Several countries are adopting multi-dimensional approaches to measure national well-being to complement traditional GDP-based metrics. Here are a few notable instances:

### 1. **European Union (EU):**
   - **Eurostat:** One of the EU’s official statistical offices provides comprehensive data through Eurostat, which includes indicators covering various socio-economic areas. These include employment rates, median household income, gender equality ratios, literacy rates, etc.
   - **Indicator List (ILP):** An important component is the European Union Statistical System (EUSS), which offers a list of core statistical indicators designed to enhance statistical consistency across member states, enabling users to track progress towards set objectives.

### 2. **Australia:**
   - **Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS):** While focused primarily on economic performance, the ABS regularly publishes supplementary information and analyses based on a wider range of indicators. Some notable ones include:
     - **Societal Cohesion Indicators:** Measures community wellbeing by analyzing crime rates, mental health status, access to justice, family stability, and child welfare outcomes.
     - **Social Inclusion Indicators:** Focuses on reducing disparities between different groups, highlighting initiatives aimed at addressing poverty, racial inequalities, and disability inclusion.
   
### 3. **Brazil:**
   - **Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE):** IBGE tracks numerous non-income-related variables to gauge national well-being effectively. Key measurements include:
     - **Economic Activity Indicator:** Measures productivity and output levels across sectors.
     - **Social Protection Index (ISP):** Tracks social assistance and welfare programs, assessing coverage and effectiveness.
     - **Regional Indicators:** Compares regional differences in living standards, noting discrepancies in infrastructure, education, and health.
  
### 4. **United States (US):**
   - **Civic Engagement Survey:** Conducted by the Census Bureau, this survey assesses individual-level civic engagement across various demographics, helping policymakers understand public participation and satisfaction with governance.
   - **Economy-wide Metrics:** US federal agencies compile comprehensive sets of national accounts (like BEA and BLS) that consider a broad spectrum of economic activity and resource allocation.
