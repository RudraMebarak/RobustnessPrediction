#### Steps:

##### 1. Identify Potential Limitations
   - Review all methods used (e.g., sample size, data collection techniques).
   - Assess if there are assumptions made that limit generalizability.
   - Look for biases that could have affected the results.

##### 2. Quantify the Limitations
   - For qualitative studies, count quotes and use statistics where applicable.
   - If quantitative, calculate mean values, standard deviations, etc.

##### 3. Explain Each Limitation
   - Provide specific reasons why each limitation exists.
   - Use clear language without jargon.

##### 4. Discuss Implications
   - How might these limitations affect your conclusions?
   - Consider alternative explanations.

##### 5. Resolve Specific Limitations
   - Propose solutions or modifications you would make if you had more resources.
   - Justify why resolving these issues is important.

##### 6. Format Your Documentation
   - Create an appendix section named "Limitations."
   - List limitations chronologically from first identification through explanation.

##### 7. Verify Completion
   - Get feedback from peers or mentors.
   - Check for logical consistency with other sections (methods vs findings).

#### Common Challenges & Solutions:

1. **Misunderstanding of Terms**:
   - Challenge: Confusion over terms like “confounding variables” or “generalization.”
   - Solution: Consult textbooks or reliable online sources; practice identifying definitions by examples.

2. **Lack of Detail**:
   - Challenge: Too few details about why something limits the study.
   - Solution: Aim to be thorough but concise. Avoid overly technical language unless necessary.

3. **Conflicting Evidence**:
   - Challenge: Finding evidence that both confirms and refutes one limitation.
   - Solution: Acknowledge contradictory evidence and explain why it’s significant and warrants further investigation.

4. **Unclear Explanation**:
   - Challenge: Difficultly conveying the severity and impact of identified limitations.
   - Solution: Use analogies or metaphors to illustrate complex ideas clearly.

5. **Incomplete Documentation**:
   - Challenge: Missing steps (e.g., quantifying limitations) that should have been performed earlier.
   - Solution: Ensure every aspect of the study is documented, even if only qualitatively at first.
