Rapid urbanization in developing countries has significant and complex environmental consequences that can have far-reaching effects on both local and global levels. Here are some key points to consider:

1. **Air Pollution**: As more people move into cities, particularly slums where infrastructure is often lacking, there's an increased risk of air pollution. This pollution originates from sources such as factories, vehicles (especially motorcycles and private cars), waste burning, and inadequate solid waste management.

2. **Water Quality Degradation**: Urbanization leads to a higher demand for water resources which can lead to over-exploitation or pollution of groundwater and surface waters. Industrial effluents and sewage contribute to this degradation, posing health risks and impacting aquatic ecosystems.

3. **Land Use Changes**: The conversion of natural habitats into urban land use contributes significantly to habitat destruction, biodiversity loss, and climate change impacts like deforestation and desertification.

4. **Energy Intensive Development**: Many developing country cities rely heavily on fossil fuels due to their limited access to renewable energy sources. This increases dependency on non-renewable energy sources and exacerbates climate change by increasing greenhouse gas emissions.

5. **Solid Waste Management Challenges**: With rapid population growth comes a surfeit of household waste. Inadequate waste collection systems can result in improper disposal methods like open dumping or burning, leading to serious health issues and contamination of an increase in household garbage and sewage production. Managing these wastes efficiently becomes increasingly difficult as the city grows.

5. Land Use Changes: Urban sprawl is a common result of rapid urbanization, which can lead to the loss of agricultural land, wetlands, forests, and wildlife habitats.

6. Greenhouse Gas Emissions: The energy used to power cities (such as electricity generated from burning fossil fuels) contributes significantly to greenhouse gas emissions, exacerbating climate change.

7. Social Inequality: While some urban areas may see improved living conditions due to rapid development, others may face overcrowding, lack of affordable housing, and social problems like crime and violence.

8. Loss of Rural Communities: Many rural communities are displaced as cities expand, resulting in cultural, social, and economic losses.

In conclusion, while rapid urbanization can bring about significant improvements in terms of economic development and infrastructure, it also poses substantial challenges related to environmental degradation, resource scarcity, and social equity.
