Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a branch of artificial intelligence that deals with how agents should take actions in an environment to maximize some notion of cumulative reward [1]. This approach has been successfully applied across various domains such as robotics, games, finance, transportation, etc.

The core idea behind RL is that an agent learns by interacting with its environment through repeated trials or observations of rewards and punishments (rewards are positive feedbacks for good behavior while punishments are negative feedbacks for bad behavior). The agent then uses this experience to update its policies - rules it follows to make decisions based on current observation - to improve future performance [2].

One fundamental assumption in RL is the concept of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), which assumes a state-space where transitions between states are probabilistic [3]. Each state corresponds to a particular situation or context, and each action corresponds to a possible choice of operation within that situation. From any given state, there exists several possible next states resulting from taking different actions, along with probabilities associated with these outcomes.

In essence, an RL agent starts in a particular state and takes one or more actions until reaching another state. In the new state, there may be a set of achievable states, and the agent can repeat the process by selecting among them again. 

A key feature of MDPs is the idea of "policy", a function that maps states to actions, guiding the agent's decision-making process at every step. An optimal policy maximizes the expected cumulative reward over all states, following and AlphaGo's victory over top-ranked player Lee Sedol in 2016.

Challenges and Future Directions:
While RL holds great promise, there are still challenges to overcome, including scalability issues, computational complexity, real-time performance requirements, ethical concerns regarding privacy and bias, and addressing catastrophic forgetting during long-term training episodes. Research areas include developing more efficient RL algorithms, improving interpretability, incorporating human feedback mechanisms, ensuring fairness and avoiding biases, designing hybrid RL frameworks combining traditional methods with AI techniques, exploring novel application domains beyond classical gaming, economics, and computer science, and fostering interdisciplinary collaborations between researchers across multiple fields. Additionally, it will be essential to develop robust legal frameworks governing the use of RL-powered technologies in critical sectors like transportation and finance.
