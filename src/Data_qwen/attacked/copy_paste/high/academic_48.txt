These laws require companies to obtain explicit consent from users, provide detailed explanations of how their data is being used, and give users control over their own data through tools like opt-out options and data portability. Non-compliance can result in hefty fines and reputational damage.

In addition to these legal requirements, many companies face internal pressures to maximize profits by exploiting customer data. This can lead to practices such as selling data to third parties without proper safeguards, or using data in ways that go beyond what users initially agreed to allow. Such actions not only violate individual rights but also undermine public confidence in the ability of organizations to use information ethically and transparently.

To address these challenges, both policymakers and industry stakeholders need to take concrete steps forward. Regulatory bodies should enforce stricter standards and penalties for non-compliance while providing guidance on best practices. Industry associations can work together to develop voluntary frameworks that promote responsible data usage and protect consumers' rights.
