### Troubleshooting Guide for Dropping Home Wi-Fi Connections

#### Prerequisites:
1. **Basic Understanding of Internet Basics:** Familiarity with what an internet connection is, how it works (e.g., wireless vs wired connections), and why sometimes they might drop or slow down.
2. **Access to Necessary Tools:** A computer with minimal technical knowledge; possibly headphones and a simple app like `Wireshark` or another similar network diagnostic tool to help trace the issue.
3. **Basic Network Awareness:** Knowledge of your home’s basic networking setup, particularly where your modem/router is located.
4. **Willingness to Take Action:**
    - **Read carefully:** This guide assumes you are looking at this guide as someone who does not know much about networks.
    - **Ask questions when needed:** Don’t hesitate to ask if something doesn’t make sense.

### Step 1: Isolate the Problem
The first step in troubleshooting dropped wi-fi connections is to figure out exactly which device is causing them. Common culprits can be devices such as smart TVs, streaming boxes, gaming consoles, etc.

**Action Steps:**
- **Identify Devices:** First, identify any new devices recently added to your home network. These could include smart appliances or other electronics.
- **Disconnect Devices:** Temporarily disable these devices one by one to see if the problem persists.

**Objective:** Sometimes cached DNS servers can interfere with connectivity.
- **Action**: Clear your browser cache and restart your internet connection using a wired Ethernet cable instead of WiFi.
