**How to Store Cooked Food Safely**

Cooking delicious meals can be a joy, but storing them correctly ensures they stay fresh longer and retain their nutritional value. Here’s a beginner-friendly guide on how to store your cooked foods:

### Prerequisites:
- Basic understanding of kitchen tools (microwave, refrigerator)
- A clean space where you won’t have to worry about cross-contamination

### Step 1: Separate Ingredients
Before cooking, ensure all ingredients are separated into different containers or dishes. This helps prevent contamination during storage and preparation.

### Step 2: Prepare Your Foods
Cut larger pieces of meat into smaller portions if possible. Cut fruits and vegetables into bite-sized chunks. If using pre-made salads or sauces, follow the manufacturer's instructions carefully.

### Step 3: Cool Down Quickly
If not eating immediately after cooking, cool down the dish quickly in an ice bath or by placing it in the refrigerator. This reduces bacterial growth.

### Step 4: Label Containers Properly
Use clear labeling with dates (e.g., "Today," "This Week," "Last Week"). Date labels help track freshness and expiration dates.

### Step 5: Use Correct Storage Containers
Choose non-reactive materials like glass or plastic containers with tight-fitting lids. Avoid metal as it can react with acidic juices from foods.

### Step 6: Refrigerate Immediately
Store cooked food in the refrigerator within two hours of cooling. For meats, use the 'danger zone' rule—do not refrigerate anything above 40°F (4°C) unless it has been defrosted first.

### Step 7: Organize Categorically
When storing multiple items, organize similar types together. Group meats, cheeses, dairy products, etc., so they don't mix and contaminate each other.

### Step 8: Check Dates Regularly
Always check the date on your stored food before serving. Discard any items past their expiry date.

### Step 9: Freeze Wisely
For long-term preservation, freeze cooked foods promptly. Wrap tightly and place in freezer-safe containers or bags. Ensure they’re frozen solid before transferring to freezer units.

### Step 10: Thaw Slowly
Thaw frozen foods slowly under cold running water or in the fridge overnight. Do **Poultry vs Beef**: Chicken breasts typically go well cooled directly into the freezer for quick reheating convenience.

#### Produce
- **Fruits & Veggies**: Store in crisper drawers first for optimal freshness retention.
- **Leafy Greens**: Wrap loosely and place on the top shelf of the fridge to aerate.
