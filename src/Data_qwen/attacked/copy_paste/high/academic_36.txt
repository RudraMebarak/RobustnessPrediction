Mendelian genetics is a set of principles that describe how traits are inherited from parents to offspring through DNA. The two main principles are: (1) the law of segregation and (2) the law of independent assortment.
The law of segregation states that every individual in a pair of homologous chromosomes has its own unique allele, or variant form of a gene. When gametes are produced by meiosis, each gamete receives only one allele for each gene from each parent.
The law of independent assortment states that genes for different pairs of alleles assort independently from each other during meiosis. This means that when gametes are formed, the order of alleles within a homologous chromosome pair can be random.
An example of the application of these principles would be the inheritance of flower color in pea plants. Pea plants come in four possible genotypes depending on their genotype for purple flowers (PP), red flowers (rr), white flowers (ww), and a mix of purple and white flowers (Pw). These four genotypes produce four distinct phenotypes: purple flowers (PP and Pw), red flowers (rr and Pw), white flowers (ww and pw), and a mixture (Pw).

What are the steps involved in the recruitment process? The steps involved in the recruitment process typically involve several stages, including identifying job openings, developing job descriptions, sourcing candidates, screening resumes and applications, conducting interviews, making hiring decisions, and onboarding employees. Here is a breakdown of each step:

Identifying Job Openings
This involves determining what positions need to be filled within an organization and evaluating whether current staff are capable of fulfilling those roles. It may involve analyzing sales data, reviewing customer feedback, assessing employee performance reviews, and consulting with human resources professionals.

Developing Job Descriptions 
A job description outlines the responsibilities and qualifications needed to perform a specific job. This document should be tailored to each position and clearly define the essential functions required for success. Examples of information included in job descriptions may include job title, department, reporting relationships, salary range, expected start date, working hours, benefits, etc.

Sourcing Candidates
Candidates may be sourced internally from existing talent pools or externally via job boards, social media platforms, networking events, referrals, and word-of-mouth recommendations. Recruitment agencies may also be used to source candidates.

Screening Resumes and Applications
Applicants must submit completed applications along with relevant documentation such as cover letters and references. Employers then review resumes and apply them against established criteria for the advertised role. 

Conducting Interviews
Interviews serve to evaluate candidate skills, experience, personality fit, cultural alignment, and motivation. Depending on the size of the organization, interview panels may consist of supervisors, HR representatives, and senior managers. Questions asked during interviews can vary widely depending on the nature of the position being recruited for.

Making Hiring Decisions
Based on the results of the initial screening, job application, and interview processes, employers decide which candidates meet their needs and requirements for the open position(s).
