Photovoltaic (PV) cells are made up of layers of semiconductor materials, typically silicon, that are doped with impurities to create p-type and n-type semiconductors. When light shines on the PV cell, it excites electrons within the atoms of the semiconductor material. This causes the electrons to move freely as if they were ions - this is called an electric current.
The direction of the electric current depends on whether the semiconductor material is p-type or n-type. In a p-n junction, the movement of electrons creates an electric field that attracts more electrons towards the negatively charged end of the cell, creating positive charges in the other direction. These opposing charges flow through the wires connected to the cell's terminals, generating electricity.
