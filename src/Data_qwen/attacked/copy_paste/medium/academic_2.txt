Evolutionarily speaking, humans have evolved primarily through changes in our DNA structure, specifically through genetic mutations. Genes within our DNA code determine characteristics like eye color, hair texture, and susceptibility to certain diseases. Over thousands or millions of years, random changes in DNA—mutations—can affect the way genes work. Some mutations lead to advantageous traits that improve survival and reproduction. These beneficial traits are then passed down genetically to offspring.

For example:
- A mutation could result in a gene producing a different protein.
- This could alter a trait that makes someone better suited for a particular environment (say, running faster).
- The next generation will inherit those advantages if they survive and reproduce successfully.

Genetic mutations aren't exclusive to humans; any species where organisms pass traits to the next generation faces similar evolutionary challenges. In the case of humans, our large brains, upright posture, language abilities, and complex societies are all adaptations resulting from genetic mutations over millennia. Without such mutations, our lineage would likely be very different today! 

So yes, genetic mutations are key drivers behind the evolution and adaptation that defines us as modern humans. They're the fundamental mechanism allowing natural selection to shape our biology and behavior over vast periods of history and present day life.
