Fluid dynamics is the study of fluids (liquids, gases) at rest and motion. The two main branches are statics and kinematics.

1. Newton's First Law: An object remains at rest or in uniform linear motion unless a net force acts on it.
2. Newton's Second Law: F=ma
3. Bernoulli's Principle: As velocity increases, pressure decreases.
4. Pascal's Principle: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid can be transmitted undiminished to every part of the fluid.
5. Torricelli's Theorem: When a right-angled triangle forms with one side parallel to water surface and lying flat at ground level, then height of liquid column formed will be equal to distance from top edge of container to highest point.

Applications:

1. Aerodynamics - Deals with flight and design of aircraft.
   * Lift - Thrust due to shape and speed
   * Drag - Resistance to movement

2. Hydrodynamics - Deals with flow in pipes or through turbines etc.
   * Flow rate Q = A × ρ × v / 2
   * Hydraulic jump occurs when pipe diameter reduces suddenly

3. Oceanography - Study of oceanic circulation and waves.
   
4. Environmental Engineering - Analyzing pollution sources like sewage, factories an ideal fluid without frictional effects.

Fluid dynamics has numerous practical applications in engineering fields, including:

1. Aerospace engineering: Understanding fluid dynamics helps engineers design aircraft wings, jet engines, and spacecraft to operate efficiently and safely in various conditions.

2. Hydraulic systems: Engineers use fluid dynamics principles to create hydraulic systems for lifting heavy objects, moving machinery, and controlling robotic devices.

3. Chemical process engineering: Fluid dynamics plays a crucial role in designing reactors, pumps, separators, filters, and other equipment used in chemical processes.

4. Environmental science: Hydrodynamics studies how fluids move through natural environments, affecting weather patterns, river flows, ocean currents, and coastal erosion.

5. Marine transportation: Engineers utilize fluid dynamics principles when designing ships, submarines, Jets vs propellers (jet engines use less fuel but produce more thrust than conventional propeller planes)
5. Floating buoys (density forces buoyant upwards)

So, fluid dynamics studies everything involved with liquids and gases at rest and motion; from everyday objects to huge spaceships. It has real-world applications everywhere you look!
