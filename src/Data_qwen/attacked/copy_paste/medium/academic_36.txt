Mendelian genetics is a set of principles that describe how traits are inherited from parents to offspring through DNA. The type contributes to the phenotype because dominance determines which allele will be expressed.

4. Law of segregation: According to this law, the alleles segregate independently of one another and assort randomly into gametes.

5. Law of independent assortment: Genes for different traits assort independently of one another during meiosis.

These principles explain how genetic variation arises in populations over time due to random mating and recombination of genetic material between generations. They also help predict how certain traits might appear in offspring based on their parents' genotypes. However, it's important to note that these principles do not fully account for all aspects of inheritance, especially complex traits involving multiple genes or environmental factors. Additionally, some mutations and changes in DNA sequence occur spontaneously, leading to new combinations of alleles even without sexual reproduction.
