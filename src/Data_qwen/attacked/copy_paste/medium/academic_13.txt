Standardized examinations, such as those used in college admissions or licensing exams, are often seen as effective tools for measuring academic ability because they provide a clear and objective way to rank students based on their performance. However, some argue that standardized examinations are not always an adequate measure of academic ability.
One reason why standardized examinations may be inadequate is because they do not take into account individual differences among learners. Students who excel in one type of learning style (e.g., visual learners) may struggle with other types (e.g., auditory learners), yet both styles are equally represented in the test-takers' demographic. This can result in unfair assessments where students are unfairly disadvantaged due to their particular strengths or weaknesses.
Another issue with standardized examinations is the lack of flexibility and adaptability.
