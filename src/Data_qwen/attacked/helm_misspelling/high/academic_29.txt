Theoretical Framework: Online Shopping Behavior (OSB)
1) Perceived Value Theory
- Consumers evaluate the value of goods and services based on perceived attributes such as quality, price, brand reputation, convenience, and product fit.
- This theory suggests that consumers are more likely to purchase products when they perceive high value relative to other options available.
2) Online Trust Model
- Consumers place trust in e-commerce platforms by observing user reviews, checking security protocols (like SSL/TLS encryption), monitoring transaction history, and verifying delivery addresses.
- This model assumes that higher levels of trust increase likelihood of completing transactions safely.
3) Social Influence Theory
- Consumers seek social proof from friends, family members, celebrities, influencers, or expert reviewers who endorse certain brands or products.
- This influence increases demand for popular items, especially during sales periods.
4) Expectations vs Reality Theory
- Consumers expect satisfaction after purchasing an item but often experience disappointment due to differences between what was advertised versus what is received. 
- Inaccuracies can lead to frustration and reduced satisfaction with the final outcome.
5) Decision-Making Styles
- Consumers engage in various stages of the decision-making process including problem identification, information gathering, evaluation criteria application, comparison selection, purchase commitment, and post-purchase behaviors.
6) Role Theory
- Customers assume diffrent roles (e.g., buyer, customer advocate, etc.) across interactions wich impact their behavior towards companies, products, and promotions.

7) Technology Acceptance Theory
- Users accept new technologies if it enhances their daily life experiences, makes tasks easier, faster, provides feedback, reduces anxiety, increases safety, or creates productivity gains while reducing costs.
8) Cognitive Dissonance Theory
- When customers perceive discrepancies between attitudes and actions taken, individuals may attempt to resolve those conflicts throught cognitive restructuring.
- This could involve searching for alternative solutions to restore balance between attitudes and behaviors.
9) Consumer Sentiment Theory
- Customer sentiment influences purchasing decisions by affecting the perception of the overall brand image and effectiveness.
- Positive sentiments boost loyalty; negative ones decrease it significantly.
10) Brand Loyalty Theory
- A sense of psychological attachment to a brand over time results in increased frequency of purchases.
- This occurs due to positive brand associations and expectations regarding future benefits derived from continued interaction with this particular company or product line.

The above theoretical framework highlights several variables influencing online shopping behavior across diffrent phases and stages of the buying cycle. Each component explains how people make choices about wich products meet thier needs most effectively and justifies why some products get sold while others do not. The combination of these theories would provide insight into developing effective strategies to increase conversion rates, improve customer satisfaction, enhance market share, and ultimately drive growth potential for e-commerce businesses operating in today's digital enviroment. 

Please note that this framework does not include every single factor impacting online shopping behavior but aims to highlight essential components needed to understand the complex dynamics behind online retailing activities at scale. Additionally, the presented theoretical models are static frameworks and do not take into account ongoing changes within the industry such as advancements in technology, evolving regulatory standards, and shifts in consumer preferences driven by demographic trends, cultural shifts, global economic conditions, climate change impacts, health and wellness focus areas, sustainability initiatives, gender equality discussions, and societal norms surrounding privacy and data management. Therefore, real-world applications should continually incorporate emerging insights and best practices to maximize performance capabilities over time. Furthermore, empirical research studies are necessary to test hypotheses, assess practicality, evaluate ROI potentials, analyze long-term buisness implications, identify strategic priorities, establish competitive landscapes, comprehend key challenges and opportunities for improvement, track progress, set realistic goals, monitor successes/failures, and adapt approaches accordingly. These analyses should help organizations shape successful operations, create innovative offerings, address current and emerging risks and threats, nurture sustainable development trajectories, align stakeholders' interests better, foster creativity among teams, improve employee engagement, enhance organizational agility, increase competitiveness, protect brand equity, reduce costs, optimize operations, attract talented personnel, build customer-centric cultures, and achieve financial stability throughout all phases of the buying journey. Also, the theoretical framework also highlights the importance of understanding the context (personal, situational, environmental, psychological, physiological factors) surrounding the individual customer making the choice to shop online, buy something specific, interact with a particular product, or use a given service.
