I remember one time when my little brother and I decided to take a trip to the beach over the summer holidays. We packed our backpacks with towels, sunscreen, flip flops, and a bucket and spade for sandcastle building.

As we walked towards the shore, there was this long line of people already gathered by the water's edge. The closer we got, the more people there were until finally, we found an open space where we could build our sandcastle safely.

My brother had been helping me find the perfect spot but as soon as I turned around, he started complaining about how tired he felt after walking all those miles.

"I've had enough!" he exclaimed. "Let's just build it here."

I knew he meant what he said - he wanted to get out of the sun and go home earlier than planned.

But you know what? I let him have his way because being a good big sister means always putting the needs of your younger sibling first.

So off we went, hand in hand along the sandy shoreline. Our sandcastle took forever to complete because we kept having fun throwing buckets of sand back at each other, pretending to be mermaids and luring imaginary sharks into traps. 

When the sandcastle was finished, we climbed on top laughing while my dad snapped photos every few seconds. The only sad thing about the whole trip was that it didn't last very long, but that wasn't a problem since we'd get more adventures like this soon! 

That trip became the highlight of our summers together as kids. Looking back on it now, I realize it was the perfect blend of adventure and carefree play that defined childhood innocence. It may seem silly or even immature to some grownups today, but to us, it was the sweetest experience ever! 

From my parents' stories years later, they told me about how upset I would get whenever my brother wouldn't stop calling me a kid (even though I was 16) and how excited I would be to keep playing on the sand dunes well past sunset. The memories are so vivid and clear now. What made this trip special for me was not just the building of a sandcastle but also making countless happy memories shared by siblings that still bring a smile to my face decades later!

It is indeed humorous looking back on childhood experiences from an adult standpoint. These stories remind us of why childhood holidays are cherished and should never be taken too seriously during our busy adulthood. After all, every child deserves quality time with loved ones, especially their brothers or sisters!
