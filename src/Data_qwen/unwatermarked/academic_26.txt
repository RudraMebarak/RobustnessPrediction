**Abstract**

This study explores the comparative performance of supervised and unsupervised machine learning (ML) algorithms in predicting stock market trends through historical financial data analysis. Utilizing a dataset comprising 10 years of daily closing prices for major global stocks, we apply both methods to identify patterns that could inform future investment decisions.

The supervised ML approach employs regression models trained on past price movements to forecast future trends based on input variables such as volume, volatility, and economic indicators. Conversely, the unsupervised method utilizes clustering techniques to group similar stock performances together without explicit guidance from external factors like previous trend predictions or labeled outcomes.

A detailed comparison between the two methodologies reveals notable differences in their predictive accuracy and interpretability. While the supervised model demonstrates high precision in accurately forecasting specific upward or downward trends within individual sectors over short-term periods, it struggles with capturing broader macroeconomic shifts and long-term systemic risks due to its reliance on linear relationships derived solely from past data.

In contrast, the unsupervised algorithm successfully identifies latent structures within the dataset, enabling insights into underlying market dynamics that may not be apparent when examining raw time series. Clustering reveals distinct groups of stocks exhibiting consistent behavior across various market conditions, suggesting potential arbitrage opportunities or areas where consolidation might occur.

However, this approach also faces challenges in maintaining consistency across different segments of the market and requires careful consideration of outliers and anomalies during analysis. Additionally, the lack of direct correlation between inputs and outputs makes interpretation more subjective compared to the clear relationship offered by traditional supervised models.

Overall, while both methods offer valuable perspectives on stock market trends, the unsupervised approach emerges as particularly potent for uncovering hidden patterns and identifying structural changes that might otherwise go unnoticed. Future research should explore integrating elements of both paradigms to develop hybrid systems capable of leveraging strengths from each to enhance overall prediction capabilities.

Keywords: Machine Learning, Supervised vs. Unsupervised, Stock Market Prediction, Historical Data Analysis
