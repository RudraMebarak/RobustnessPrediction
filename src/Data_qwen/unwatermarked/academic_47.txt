Discuss how alternative measures such as the Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI) or Total Welfare Index could provide a more comprehensive view of societal well-being, while also considering potential criticisms and drawbacks associated with these alternatives.
The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is often considered the most widely used measure of national economic performance in the world today. However, there are several limitations to relying solely on GDP as the primary indicator of national development.

One major limitation of GDP is that it does not take into account non-market activities or the distribution of income within society. For example, if a country's economy relies heavily on the informal sector where many people work without formal employment contracts, their contributions to GDP may be overlooked. Additionally, GDP doesn't reflect changes in quality of life - for instance, improvements in healthcare, education, and social services don’t show up when calculating GDP growth.

Furthermore, GDP can mask problems like environmental degradation or income inequality which have significant impacts on human well-being but aren't captured by this statistic alone. In other words, GDP might indicate rapid growth but hide underlying issues related to sustainability and fairness.

To address these shortcomings, some economists advocate for the use of broader indicators such as the Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI), developed by Alkemade et al., or the Total Welfare Index, proposed by Gwartney & Lawson.

The GPI measures actual welfare gains made through production rather than just monetary output, taking into consideration factors such as health care costs, pollution control, and resource depletion. It calculates what the total cost would be under current policies to restore natural capital lost due to environmental damage. The GPI provides a clearer picture of the true progress of an economy by accounting for both positive and negative externalities.

On the other hand, while these indices offer valuable insights beyond traditional GDP metrics, they face their own challenges. Critics argue that the GPI may overemphasize ecological concerns at the expense of economic efficiency, leading to inefficient allocation of resources. Moreover, implementing the GPI requires detailed knowledge about local conditions and market structures, making it difficult to apply universally.

The Total Welfare Index proposes combining multiple components reflecting various aspects of well-being, including consumption levels, leisure time, personal safety, and access to basic goods and services. Although theoretically appealing, this approach faces practical hurdles given data availability constraints and differing definitions across countries.

In conclusion, while GDP remains crucial for tracking overall economic activity, its limitations underscore the need for complementary measures. By incorporating alternative perspectives offered by GPI and TWI, societies can achieve a fuller understanding of societal development. However, integrating such holistic frameworks requires careful consideration of methodology and policy implications to ensure effective implementation. Ultimately, achieving sustainable prosperity necessitates a multifaceted evaluation of economic outcomes alongside social and environmental considerations.

#### 1. **Employee Information Management**
   - **Personal Data**: Centralized storage of personal details such as names, addresses, contact numbers, and email addresses.
   - **Policies and Procedures**: Documentation of company policies, guidelines, and procedures for various HR functions.

   - **Candidate Management System**: Tools for sourcing candidates, scheduling interviews, managing job applications, and candidate follow-ups.
   - **Onboarding Modules**: Automated processes for new hires, including orientation schedules, introductions to colleagues, and integration with existing software solutions.

#### 3. **Performance Management**
   - **Goal Setting and Tracking**: Automated goal-setting tools, regular feedback mechanisms, and continuous monitoring of individual and team performance against set objectives.
