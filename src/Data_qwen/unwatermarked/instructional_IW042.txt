**Beginner-Friendly Guide: Documenting Limitations in Small Research Studies**

#### Prerequisites:
- Basic understanding of your chosen topic
- Familiarity with basic scientific writing conventions (APA or another style)
- Access to reliable sources for information

---

### Step 1: Identify Potential Limitations
Before you can begin documenting limitations, it's essential to identify what they are. Start by reviewing any literature related to your research question. Look for studies that have explored similar topics and see if there were any gaps or issues they faced.

### Step 2: Understand Your Study Design
Review your research design—whether it was an experiment, survey, observational study, etc. Understanding the type of study helps pinpoint potential issues such as sample size, methodology, data collection methods, and external factors influencing outcomes.

### Step 3: List Key Issues Identified
Create a list of potential limitations based on the above steps. This should include both known and potential future challenges.

### Step 4: Explain Each Issue
For each limitation identified, write a brief explanation about why this is important and what could potentially affect the validity or reliability of your findings. Be specific about the nature of these issues and how they might impact your results.

### Step 5: Discuss How These Limitations Might Affect Interpretation
Consider how these limitations might influence how other researchers interpret your results. Discuss whether these limitations are significant enough to warrant caution when applying your findings broadly.

### Step 6: Propose Solutions or Recommendations
Based on the identified limitations, suggest solutions or recommendations for addressing them. For example, increasing the sample size, using more robust data analysis techniques, or exploring different variables.

### Step 7: Cite Relevant Sources
When discussing limitations, cite the original sources where you found information relevant to your discussion. Ensure you're referencing credible, peer-reviewed articles.

### Step 8: Review and Revise
After completing your list and explanations, review everything carefully. Check for consistency across your paper and ensure that all limitations mentioned are addressed appropriately.

### Step 9: Finalize Your Paper
Once satisfied with your documentation, finalize your research paper by incorporating your limitations section into your main text, ensuring that every limitation is discussed and addressed.

### Verification:
To verify the completion of this process, ask peers or mentors to review your work. They can provide feedback on clarity, organization, and adherence to scientific writing standards.

By following these steps, you'll effectively document the limitations of your small research study, enhancing its credibility and integrity through transparency. # The Effects of Social Media on Mental Health

## Increased Stress Levels
One of the most apparent effects of social media use is the increase in stress levels. Social media often promotes comparisons between users' lives and their own, which can lead to feelings of inadequacy and low self-esteem. Users may compare themselves to popular figures on social media who seem happier than they are, leading to feelings of envy and anxiety.

## Decreased Empathy
Social media platforms often present idealized versions of life, highlighting happy moments and accomplishments without mentioning the struggles and hardships that come with living. This can lead to a decrease in empathy towards others. When individuals only see the best parts of someone else's life, they may become less able to understand and relate to those facing difficult circumstances.

## Depression
The constant exposure to positive messages and content on social media can contribute to depression symptoms. When users feel like they are missing out on life experiences due to not having enough followers, likes, or comments, they may start to develop a sense of dissatisfaction with their own lives. Additionally, the fear of judgment and criticism on social media can exacerbate feelings of loneliness and isolation, contributing to overall mood swings and depressive symptoms.
