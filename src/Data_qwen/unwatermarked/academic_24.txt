Potential limitations or challenges include:

1. Response bias: Due to the random sampling method, there may be certain groups of students (e.g., older adults) who might not respond to the survey, leading to biased results.

2. Questionnaire length: If the survey is too long, it could lead to fatigue and low completion rates, potentially reducing the reliability and validity of the findings.

3. Social desirability bias: Participants may provide socially desirable answers rather than honest responses, especially if they feel that their opinions matter.

Strategies to overcome these challenges include:

1. Ensuring anonymity: Offering anonymity can help reduce social desirability bias and encourage more honest responses.

2. Reducing questionnaire length: Limiting the number of questions can make the survey shorter and easier to complete, thus increasing response rates.

3. Pilot testing: Conducting pilot tests with a small group of students before full-scale implementation can help identify and address potential issues.

4. Recruitment efforts: Employing targeted recruitment methods (e.g., leveraging social media, email campaigns) can increase participation rates among underrepresented populations.

5. Providing incentives: Offering small rewards or certificates for completing the survey can motivate participation.
