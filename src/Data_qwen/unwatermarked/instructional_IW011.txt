Evaluating Sources: A Beginner's Guide

Prerequisites:
- Familiarity with basic research concepts
- Understanding of plagiarism and ethical writing practices
- Basic computer literacy (accessing internet resources)

Step 1: Identify the type of source you need
Before starting your search, determine what kind of information you're looking for. This will help narrow down your options and focus on reliable sources.

Step 2: Define your keywords or topic
Clearly define the subject matter of your research project. Use specific terms that accurately describe the area of study, including any subtopics or nuances that are important for your paper.

Step 3: Search online databases
Use keyword searches in reputable online databases such as JSTOR, Google Scholar, Academic Search Premier, or ProQuest. Type your defined keywords into the database's search bar and limit results by publication date if needed.

Step 4: Read through abstracts and introductions
Look at the abstracts and introductions of articles to get an idea of their content and author credentials. Pay attention to whether they cite other relevant works within the field.

Step 5: Evaluate the author(s)
Examine each author's background and qualifications related to the topic. Look for authors who have published extensively in this field and whose work is cited frequently by other experts. Assess their expertise level by checking their credentials on publications websites like ResearchGate or Academia.edu.

Step 6: Check the publisher/website
Verify the legitimacy of the publisher or website hosting the article. Ensure it has been reviewed and approved by a professional body or organization known for quality standards in academia. Avoid sites affiliated with commercial entities or those without clear editorial policies.

Step 7: Examine references
Evaluate the number and relevance of the article's citations to other studies. Stronger arguments often rely on multiple supporting pieces of evidence rather than just one source. Be wary of poorly constructed citations which may indicate incomplete research efforts.

Pitfalls to avoid:
- Using unverified or biased sources
- Plagiarizing from others' papers without proper citation
- Relying solely on personal opinions instead of factual evidence 
- Failing to assess the credibility of different types of sources (e.g., primary vs. secondary sources) 

Completion verification:
To ensure thorough evaluation, compare your findings against the required citation style format (APA, MLA, Chicago etc.) and cross-check the references against the bibliography provided by the source itself. Double-check all steps taken during the process for accuracy and completeness.
