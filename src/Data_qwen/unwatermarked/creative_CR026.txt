The characters are detectives investigating the crime, and one of them must break into the room to retrieve the evidence. Write in third person limited point of view, focusing on the detective who is about to enter the room and the tension building around them.

Detective Sarah had been working for hours trying to solve the case, but she was beginning to lose her patience. Her partner, Jack, had just arrived at the crime scene with new leads, and they were ready to move forward once again. But as they entered the closed-off room, something felt off – the air was stale and there was an eerie silence hanging over everything inside.

Sarah's heart racing, she took a deep breath before stepping towards the door. She pushed it open cautiously, her eyes scanning the room frantically. There was nothing out of place, no fingerprints or footprints on the walls or floor. Just empty space.

"Jack," she called out, "we need to get this evidence."

The detective turned to face him, her voice steady despite the nerves twisting in her stomach. "I'm coming with you," she said firmly.

He nodded, his expression grim. Together, they began to search every inch of the room, their hands moving swiftly through drawers and filing cabinets. Sarah found what she was looking for - a small envelope sealed with wax seal, its contents barely visible from behind the dust cover. 

As they lifted the lid, a chill ran down Sarah's spine. Inside was a single letter written in blood, ink splattered across the page in random strokes. It was dated only two days ago - too recent to be the work of a murderer who had already fled the area. But who could have done such a thing? And why?

They continued searching, each moment longer than the last. Finally, after what seemed like an eternity, they found what they needed: a fingerprint match from the victim's home address, leading back to the suspect in question. But now, with the evidence safely in hand, they knew they'd finally cracked the case.

But as Sarah stepped outside, closing the door behind her, she couldn't shake the feeling that someone else was watching them... waiting...

And then, suddenly, she heard footsteps echoing up the stairs nearby. Someone was approaching, fast, determined. The fear gripped her tightly. If they weren't careful, they might never see justice served. Not yet.

With a heavy sigh, she turned to Jack, determination burning in her eyes. They wouldn't give up until they caught whoever did this. And if they had to break into the room themselves, they would do so without hesitation. Because time was running out, and justice demanded their attention. 

She reached for the handle, prepared to step inside once more when a sudden gust of wind blew open the door, knocking Sarah off balance. In the doorway stood a shadowy figure - a man dressed in black, hood pulled low over his head. His eyes glowed red under the streetlight casting a sickly yellow glow upon him.

Sarah froze where she stood, paralyzed by fear. The other detective looked at her, confusion etched onto his features.

"What?" he asked softly, concern evident in his voice.

Before either of them could react, the man lunged forward, grabbing hold of Jack's arm and pulling him towards himself. Sarah watched in horror as her colleague stumbled backwards, his body twisted awkwardly beneath the weight of whatever force was being used against him. Blood gushed from his forehead, staining his clothes. The attacker raised a gloved hand, fingers curling around the edge of the table top, squeezing hard enough to make Sarah wince.

"You're going to pay for this," the man snarled, his voice dripping with malice. "You will not escape me..."

For the first time since entering the room, Sarah realized how close they came to losing Jack forever. How many lives depended on them finding the culprit quickly and efficiently. She knew they couldn't let anything happen to him; not now, not ever. With newfound resolve, she grabbed the handle and pushed open the door again, turning to face the attacker. This time, she wasn't afraid anymore. This time, she'd take control.
