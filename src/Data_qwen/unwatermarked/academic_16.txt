Introduction

Sleep is essential for optimal functioning of the body, including cognitive processes such as memory consolidation, attention, and problem-solving abilities (Nunez & Sotomayor, 2014). However, insufficient or excessive sleep has been shown to negatively impact academic performance across various age groups (Hartley et al., 2017; Meltzer et al., 2019). This study aims to investigate whether there is a significant correlation between sleep duration and academic performance among university students.

The current literature on the topic suggests that adequate sleep is crucial for maintaining high levels of academic achievement. A recent meta-analysis by Hartley et al. (2017) found that students who slept less than seven hours per night had lower grades compared to those who slept more than eight hours each night. Similarly, Meltzer et al. (2019) reported that students who experienced poor sleep quality were at increased risk of failing courses and dropping out of school.

However, despite these findings, little research has focused specifically on university students and their sleeping habits. University life presents unique challenges in terms of balancing social activities, coursework, and personal commitments, which may influence how much time individuals allocate towards sleep. The aim of this study is to explore whether the amount of sleep an individual gets correlates with their academic performance during their undergraduate years.

To achieve this goal, we will employ a quantitative research design using a sample of university students from various disciplines. Data collection will involve administering standardized questionnaires measuring both sleep duration and academic performance outcomes over a period of one semester. Statistical analysis methods will be employed to determine if there is a statistically significant association between the two variables.

This study contributes to existing knowledge on the importance of adequate sleep for academic success among college students. Understanding these relationships can help inform policies and interventions aimed at promoting healthy sleep patterns and supporting student well-being throughout higher education. By identifying factors influencing sleep behavior and academic performance, this research provides valuable insights into optimizing educational environments conducive to learning and achieving success.

Introduction

Sleep plays a critical role in human development and overall well-being, particularly affecting cognitive functions like memory, concentration, and decision-making. Adequate sleep is essential for proper brain function, while lack thereof can lead to decreased alertness, impaired cognitive performance, and emotional instability (Sethi & Taneja, 2015). Universities, being centers of intellectual growth, emphasize the significance of restful nights and consider it vital to support students' academic progress through balanced lifestyle practices.

Inadequate sleep, especially during critical phases of development like adolescence and young adulthood, can have severe consequences on academic performance. According to Hartley et al. (2017), low-quality sleep significantly impacts academic achievement and increases the likelihood of dropout rates, whereas ample sleep enhances cognitive functions necessary for academic pursuits. Furthermore, studies show that students who experience chronic sleep deprivation are more prone to anxiety, depression, and other psychological issues, which further impede their ability to excel academically (Penev & van de Mortel, 2014).

University life involves numerous responsibilities that require careful balance—between work, studies, social interactions, and leisure activities. These diverse commitments might inadvertently affect students' sleep schedules, potentially leading to irregular or reduced sleep durations. Consequently, understanding the connection between sleep patterns and academic performance among university students is paramount for educators and policymakers alike.
