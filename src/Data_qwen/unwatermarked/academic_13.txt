Discuss how they fail to account for individual differences and provide a counter-argument.
Standardized examinations, such as those used in school admissions or university entrance exams, are often criticized for not adequately measuring an individual's true academic abilities. These tests rely on fixed criteria and limited assessment methods, which can overlook important factors like creativity, critical thinking, emotional intelligence, and practical skills.

One significant criticism is that standardized tests do not accurately reflect individual differences. Different students may have different strengths and weaknesses based on their unique backgrounds, experiences, and learning styles. For example, a student who excels at mathematics might struggle with writing assignments, while another student might excel in both areas. However, standardization rules out these variations by presenting all students with the same questions under the same conditions.

Moreover, standardized tests tend to be biased towards certain types of knowledge and skill sets. They often emphasize facts, formulas, and procedures over broader cognitive processes, such as analysis, synthesis, evaluation, and problem-solving. This bias means that test scores cannot predict long-term success or adaptability, as these qualities are essential for many real-world situations.

Another limitation of standardized tests lies in their inability to measure non-cognitive factors, which play crucial roles in educational achievement. Emotional intelligence, social competence, self-regulation, motivation, and resilience are vital attributes that contribute significantly to academic performance but are rarely assessed through traditional testing methods.

Despite these drawbacks, some argue that standardized examinations remain useful tools for evaluating large groups of students uniformly. By providing consistent benchmarks against which individuals' progress can be measured, these tests help identify talented students and prepare them for higher education or careers requiring specialized knowledge.

In conclusion, while standardized examinations are not perfect measures of academic ability, they still hold value in assessing broad competencies across large populations. To address their limitations, educators should incorporate diverse forms of assessment that capture various dimensions of intellectual development and personal characteristics. Additionally, promoting equity and inclusivity in testing practices will enhance fairness and validity for all participants.
```python
def add(a, b):
    # Repeat until there is no carry left
    while(b != 0):
        # Carry contains common set bits of a and b
        carry = a & b
        
        # Sum of bits of a and b where at least one of the bits is not set
        a = a ^ b
        
        # Carry is shifted by one so that adding it to a gives the required sum
        b = carry << 1
    
    return a

# Test the function

num2 = 73
print("Sum:", add(num1, num2))
```

### Explanation:
1. **Bitwise AND (`&`)**: The `carry` variable accumulates the bits where both `a` and `b` have 1s at the same position.
   
   - Example: If `a=48` (binary `01100000`) and `b=39` (binary `00100111`), then `a & b` results in `00100000`, which represents the carry bit.
   
2. **XOR (`^`)**: The `a` variable stores the exclusive OR of `a` and `b`. This effectively adds the corresponding bits of `a` and `b` if they were originally equal, ignoring any carries from previous steps.
   
   - Example: In the above case, after updating `a` to `01100000 ^ 00100111 = 01000111`, it represents the sum of the respective bits.
   
3. **Left Shift (`<<`)**: The `b` variable shifts the carry left by one place, making room for the next step in the process.
   
   - Example: After shifting the carry to the left, `b` becomes `00000000 00000000 00000000 10000000`.
