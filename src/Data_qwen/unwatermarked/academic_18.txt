**Abstract**

This study investigates the impact of renewable energy adoption on urban sustainability by comparing two groups: "Green Cities" (which prioritize renewable energy) and "Non-Green Cities" (which do not). Over an 18-month period, Green Cities implemented significant renewable energy initiatives including solar panels, wind turbines, and geothermal systems. Non-Green Cities maintained their traditional energy infrastructure with fossil fuels as the primary power source.

The study's key findings indicate:
- **Energy Efficiency**: Green Cities saw a substantial reduction in greenhouse gas emissions due to higher efficiency levels achieved through renewable energy.
- **Economic Growth**: There was a noticeable increase in job creation within the renewable sector and overall economic growth linked to sustainable practices.
- **Environmental Quality**: Air quality improved significantly in Green Cities due to reduced pollution from fossil fuel usage.
- **Community Engagement**: Community participation increased dramatically in both cities; however, Green Cities experienced more robust support networks and local government cooperation.

Despite these positive outcomes, the study also reveals challenges such as initial costs of transitioning to renewables, public resistance to change, and variability in policy implementation across different regions. Future studies aim to explore strategies for overcoming these barriers and maximizing the benefits of renewable energy adoption in urban areas.
