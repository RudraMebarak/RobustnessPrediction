Theoretical Framework: Online Shopping Behavior (OSB)

1. Perceived Value Theory:
   - Consumers evaluate the perceived value of an online shopping experience based on factors like product quality, pricing, delivery speed, and user interface.
   - This theory posits that consumers will make purchasing decisions when the perceived value exceeds their cost-benefit analysis.

2. Social Influence Theory:
   - Consumer behavior is significantly influenced by peer and social networks' recommendations and opinions.
   - Online shoppers are more likely to purchase from trusted brands or follow popular trends within the community.

3. Cognitive Dissonance Theory:
   - Consumers may feel uncomfortable when they perceive inconsistencies between their beliefs about products/services and actual experiences.
   - To reduce dissonance, individuals adjust their perceptions or behaviors.

4. Expectancy-Value Model:
   - Consumers compare the expected benefits (product features) with the anticipated costs (time, effort, money).
   - They choose options where the expected benefits exceed the costs, considering the overall satisfaction level.

5. Resource Dependence Theory:
   - In a competitive environment, consumers rely on external resources (e.g., information, reviews, promotions) to make informed decisions.
   - These resources influence consumer behavior, affecting purchase intentions and outcomes.

6. Technology Acceptance Model:
   - Consumers assess the ease of use and usefulness of new technologies before adopting them into their daily routines.
   - Factors influencing technology acceptance include perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and technological knowledge.

7. Motivation-Dominant Process Model:
   - Consumer motivation drives their decision-making process through internal and external stimuli.
   - Motivational processes encompass cognitive, affective, and conative components.

8. Risk-Aversion Hypothesis:
   - Some consumers exhibit higher risk aversion, preferring low-risk alternatives over high-risk ones.
   - Their preferences shape consumption patterns, particularly in risky online shopping scenarios.

9. Self-Efficacy Theory:
   - Consumers have varying levels of confidence in their ability to perform certain tasks or handle situations.
   - Self-efficacy influences online shopping behavior, impacting trust, willingness to try new services, and reliance on specific platforms.

10. Information Processing Theory:
    - Consumers evaluate information systematically to make rational choices.
    - Effective communication channels, clear presentation of data, and accessible search functions can enhance consumer understanding and satisfaction.

This theoretical framework aims to provide a comprehensive overview of various psychological and behavioral theories related to online shopping behavior, offering insights into how consumers interact with e-commerce sites, engage in purchases, and ultimately derive satisfaction or dissatisfaction from these experiences. Understanding these frameworks is crucial for businesses aiming to design effective strategies, improve customer engagement, and maximize sales in online shopping environments. 

Note: This theoretical framework is not exhaustive and other models and theories may also be relevant depending on the research focus and context. However, it provides a broad base of ideas to explore in further studies on online shopping behavior. For instance, researchers could investigate the intersection of these theories or develop hybrid models incorporating elements from different frameworks to better capture complex real-world phenomena. Human Resource Management (HRM) plays a critical role in driving organizational success by addressing key areas such as employee recruitment, retention, development, and compensation. Here’s how HRM contributes to organizational success:

### 1. **Employee Recruitment**
HRM helps organizations attract top talent by implementing effective recruitment strategies. This includes sourcing candidates from diverse pools, using job descriptions that clearly outline job requirements and responsibilities, and leveraging digital tools to reach broader audiences. Efficient recruitment ensures that organizations have access to qualified applicants who align with their business needs.

### 2. **Employee Retention**
Effective HR practices help retain employees by fostering a positive work culture, providing fair and transparent compensation packages, promoting career development opportunities, and ensuring open lines of communication. By focusing on employee well-being, HR can create a workplace environment that attracts and retains top performers, reducing turnover rates and associated costs.

### 3. **Employee Development**
HRM supports continuous learning and skill enhancement among employees. This is achieved through training programs, mentorship initiatives, performance coaching, and encouraging professional growth through cross-functional rotations or specialized courses. A well-developed employee learning system enhances productivity, innovation, and adaptability, which are essential for long-term organizational success.

### 4. **Compensation and Benefits**
Competitive salaries, attractive bonus structures, and robust benefit packages motivate employees to perform at their best while maintaining balance between personal life and professional commitments. Properly designed compensation and benefits packages reflect market standards, meet legal compliance, and incentivize consistent contributions from employees towards achieving organizational goals.

### 5. **Workplace Safety and Health**
Implementing health and safety protocols reduces injury risks and promotes a healthy workforce.
