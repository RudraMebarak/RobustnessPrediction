Can we really learn by just watching others do things, or do we need hands-on experience?

While it's true that some people find tutorials to be incredibly helpful, others may find that they need hands-on experience to truly learn something. The answer is not a simple yes or no, as it depends on the individual and what they are trying to learn. However, here are some arguments for both sides:

**Arguments for watching tutorials being useful:**

1. **Accessibility**: Tutorials can be accessed at any time and from anywhere, making them a convenient way to learn new skills.
2. **Cost-effective**: Tutorials can be a cost-effective way to learn new skills, especially for those who can't afford to take classes or workshops.
3. **Structured learning**: Tutorials can provide a structured learning experience, with clear instructions and a step-by-step approach.

**Arguments for hands-on experience being more useful:**

1. **Immediacy**: Hands-on experience allows learners to see the results of their actions immediately, making it easier to understand and retain new skills.
2. **Practical application**: Hands-on experience allows learners to apply new skills to real-world situations, making it easier to apply what they've learned.
3. **Transferability**: Hands-on experience can help learners transfer what they've learned to different contexts and situations.

Ultimately, whether watching tutorials is as useful as learning by doing depends on the individual and what they are trying to learn. Some people may find that tutorials are sufficient for learning new skills, while others may need hands-on experience to truly understand and apply what they've learned.

However, here are some scenarios where watching tutorials may be sufficient:

* **Basic skills**: For basic skills like typing, using a computer, or basic cooking techniques, tutorials can be sufficient.
* **Theory-based subjects**: For theory-based subjects like mathematics, science, or language, tutorials can provide a solid foundation and help learners understand complex concepts.
* **Repetitive tasks**: For repetitive tasks like data entry or formatting documents, tutorials can be sufficient to help learners get the hang of it.

On the other hand, here are some scenarios where hands-on experience is likely to be more useful:

* **Creative pursuits**: For creative pursuits like painting, writing, or photography, hands-on experience is likely to be more valuable.
* **Complex skills**: For complex skills like programming, web development, or engineering, hands-on experience is often necessary to truly understand and master.
* **Real-world applications**: For real-world applications like project management, business operations, or social work, hands-on experience is likely to be more valuable.

In conclusion, while tutorials can be a useful starting point for learning new skills, hands-on experience is often more valuable for truly understanding and applying what you've learned. Ultimately, the best approach depends on the individual and what they are trying to learn.
