**Prerequisites:**

* Familiarity with basic computer hardware and software
* Comfortable workspace setup
* Basic understanding of ergonomic principles

**Objective:**

By following this guide, you will be able to create a well-lit and ergonomic workspace that promotes productivity, comfort, and visual clarity.

**Preparation Steps:**

1. **Assess your workspace:**
	* Walk through your home workspace and identify any areas that require improvement (e.g., lighting, ergonomics).
	* Consider the room's natural lighting, furniture, and any obstructions.
2. **Determine the lighting needs:**
	* Calculate the required lighting levels for your workspace (e.g., task lighting, ambient lighting).
	* Decide on the type of lighting fixtures (e.g., overhead, desk lamps, under-cabinet lighting).
3. **Choose ergonomic furniture:**
	* Select a comfortable chair and desk that fit your body and work style.
	* Consider a sit-stand desk or adjustable desk height.
4. **Optimize your workspace layout:**
	* Arrange your workspace to promote good posture, reduce eye strain, and improve air circulation.
	* Use a comfortable, ergonomic keyboard tray and mouse rest.
5. **Add visual elements:**
	* Incorporate plants, artwork, or other visual stimuli to create a visually appealing workspace.
	* Use color-coding or organization systems to keep your workspace organized.
6. **Verify completion:**
	* Conduct a walk-through to ensure your workspace meets the established criteria.
	* Make any necessary adjustments to lighting, ergonomics, or visual elements.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**

**Assess your workspace:**

1. Walk through your home workspace and identify areas that require improvement.
2. Take note of the room's natural lighting, furniture, and any obstructions.
3. Consider the following factors:
	* Natural lighting: Do you have enough light to work effectively? Is the light source comfortable?
	* Furniture: Is the chair and desk comfortable? Are there any obstacles or hazards?
	* Obstructions: Are there any cluttered areas or tripping hazards?
4. Take photos or videos of your workspace to reference later.

**Determine the lighting needs:**

1. Calculate the required lighting levels for your workspace.
2. Decide on the type of lighting fixtures (e.g., overhead, desk lamps, under-cabinet lighting).
3. Consider the following factors:
	* Task lighting: Do you need to focus on specific tasks (e.g., reading, coding)?
	* Ambient lighting: Do you need to create a comfortable atmosphere (e.g., a cozy workspace)?

**Choose ergonomic furniture:**

1. Select a comfortable chair and desk that fit your body and work style.
2. Consider a sit-stand desk or adjustable desk height.
3. Look for the following features:
	* Comfortable seating height
	* Adjustable armrests
	* Supportive lumbar roll
	* Ergonomic keyboard tray and mouse rest

**Optimize your workspace layout:**

1. Arrange your workspace to promote good posture, reduce eye strain, and improve air circulation.
2. Use a comfortable, ergonomic keyboard tray and mouse rest.
3. Consider the following factors:
	* Position your monitor directly in front of you, at a comfortable distance.
	* Keep your keyboard and mouse close to your body.
	* Position your chair at a comfortable height.

**Add visual elements:**

1. Incorporate plants, artwork, or other visual stimuli to create a visually appealing workspace.
2. Use color-coding or organization systems to keep your workspace organized.
3. Consider the following factors:
	* Add plants to improve air quality and aesthetics.
	* Hang artwork or prints to create a visually appealing atmosphere.
	* Use color-coding systems to categorize and organize your files or projects.

**Verify completion:**

1. Conduct a walk-through to ensure your workspace meets the established criteria.
2. Make any necessary adjustments to lighting, ergonomics, or visual elements.
3. Take photos or videos of your workspace to reference later.

**Final Check:**

1. Review your workspace to ensure it meets the established criteria.
2. Make any necessary adjustments to lighting, ergonomics, or visual elements.
3. Take a step back and evaluate your workspace as a whole.
