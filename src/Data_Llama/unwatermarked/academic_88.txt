Behavioral economics, also known as behavioral finance, is an interdisciplinary field that combines insights from psychology, sociology, economics, and computer science to understand human behavior and decision-making.

Theoretical foundations of behavioral economics:

1. **Cognitive Biases**: Behavioral economists recognize that people are prone to cognitive biases, such as confirmation bias, anchoring bias, and availability heuristic, which can lead to irrational decisions.
2. **Loss Aversion**: Behavioral economists argue that people tend to prefer avoiding losses to acquiring gains, which is a fundamental principle of classical economics.
3. **Herd Behavior**: Behavioral economists study how social norms and groupthink can influence individual decision-making.
4. **Emotions and Affect**: Behavioral economists recognize the significant role of emotions in decision-making, particularly in the context of financial markets.
5. **Anchoring and Framing Effects**: Behavioral economists study how people respond to anchoring (e.g., the initial value of a product) and framing effects (e.g., the way information is presented).
6. **Social Norms and Culture**: Behavioral economists examine how social norms and cultural values influence individual behavior.

Challenges to classical economic assumptions:

1. **Individual vs. Collective Behavior**: Behavioral economics highlights the differences between individual and collective behavior, where the former is more influential in shaping financial decisions.
2. **Emotional Decision-Making**: Behavioral economics challenges the idea that rational, emotional decision-making is always superior to rational, analytical decision-making.
3. **Contextual Effects**: Behavioral economics recognizes that context plays a significant role in shaping financial decisions, such as the impact of social norms, cultural values, and environmental factors.
4. **Limited Information**: Behavioral economics acknowledges that individuals may have limited information about the market or investment opportunities, leading to irrational decisions.
5. **Non-Rational Decision-Making**: Behavioral economics challenges the assumption that decision-making is always rational and based on objective facts.

By understanding the theoretical foundations of behavioral economics and its challenges to classical economic assumptions, we can better appreciate the complexities of human behavior and decision-making in financial markets.
