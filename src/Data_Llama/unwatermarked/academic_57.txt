An algorithmic decision-making system is one that can be programmed to make decisions based on rules, data, and other inputs. In general, algorithms are deterministic, meaning they produce the same output for the same input. However, the nature of decision-making can vary significantly depending on the context, data, and human judgment. Therefore, can an algorithmic decision-making system be considered objective?

## Step 1: Understanding the concept of objectivity in decision-making
Objectivity in decision-making refers to the quality of being unbiased, impartial, and based on facts rather than personal opinions or emotions. In the context of algorithmic decision-making systems, objectivity can be challenging to achieve, especially when dealing with complex or nuanced data.

## Step 2: Examining the determinism of algorithms
Algorithms are deterministic, meaning they follow a set of rules and produce a specific output for a given input. However, this determinism can be misleading, as it implies that the algorithm is completely objective, regardless of the data or context. In reality, algorithms can be influenced by various factors, such as biases in the data, human interpretation, or external factors like external noise.

## Step 3: Considering the role of human judgment in decision-making
Human judgment plays a significant role in decision-making, and algorithms are no exception. While algorithms can process vast amounts of data, they are still limited by their programming and the data they are given. Human judgment can introduce errors, biases, or inconsistencies that can affect the outcome of an algorithmic decision.

## Step 4: Evaluating the impact of context and data on decision-making
The context and data used to train an algorithm can significantly impact the outcome of its decision. For example, an algorithm designed to make decisions based on weather patterns may produce different results depending on the time of year or the specific weather conditions. This highlights the importance of considering the context and data when evaluating the objectivity of an algorithmic decision-making system.

## Step 5: Conclusion
In conclusion, while algorithms can be programmed to make decisions based on rules and data, they are not necessarily objective. The determinism of algorithms, human judgment, context, and data can all introduce biases and inconsistencies that affect the outcome of an algorithmic decision. Therefore, an algorithmic decision-making system can be considered subjective, rather than objective.

The final answer is: $\boxed{No}$
