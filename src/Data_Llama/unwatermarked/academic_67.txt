## Step 1: Understanding Qualitative Research Designs
Qualitative research designs are used to collect and analyze data that is subjective, non-numerical, and open-ended. They aim to provide a detailed understanding of a phenomenon, often through in-depth interviews, focus groups, and observations. The primary goal is to gain insight into the underlying structures, meanings, and experiences of a social phenomenon.

## Step 2: Understanding Quantitative Research Designs
Quantitative research designs are used to collect and analyze data that is numerical, numerical, and numerical. They aim to provide a clear understanding of a phenomenon through statistical analysis and data visualization. The primary goal is to establish a clear cause-and-effect relationship between variables.

## Step 3: Understanding Mixed-Method Research Designs
Mixed-methods research designs combine qualitative and quantitative methods to collect and analyze data. This approach aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of a phenomenon by combining the strengths of both qualitative and quantitative methods.

## Step 4: Comparison of Research Designs
- **Strengths and Limitations of Each Design:**
  - **Qualitative Research Designs:** Strengths include providing rich, detailed data, allowing for in-depth exploration of a phenomenon. Limitations include being subjective and time-consuming.
  - **Quantitative Research Designs:** Strengths include providing a clear, objective measure of a phenomenon, allowing for statistical analysis and data visualization. Limitations include being numerical and limited to a specific population.
  - **Mixed-Method Research Designs:** Strengths include providing a balanced approach, allowing researchers to leverage the strengths of both qualitative and quantitative methods. Limitations include the complexity of combining different methods and the need for careful analysis.

## Step 5: Conclusion
In conclusion, each research design has its unique strengths and limitations. Qualitative research designs are ideal for exploring complex, subjective phenomena, while quantitative research designs are suited for establishing cause-and-effect relationships and providing numerical data.
