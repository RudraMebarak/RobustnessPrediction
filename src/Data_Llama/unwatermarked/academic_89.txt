## Step 1: Define scientific falsifiability
Scientific falsifiability refers to the ability of a scientific theory to be proven false by empirical evidence. In other words, it is the property of a theory that it can be tested and found to be incorrect.

## Step 2: Explain the concept of falsifiability in the context of scientific theories
A scientific theory is considered falsifiable if it is possible to design an experiment or observation that could lead to its rejection. This means that if a theory is found to be false, it must be because the evidence presented contradicts it.

## Step 3: Discuss the role of scientific falsifiability in distinguishing scientific theories from non-scientific claims
Scientific falsifiability is a critical component in distinguishing scientific theories from non-scientific claims. If a theory is not falsifiable, it cannot be tested or proven wrong, and it is not subject to peer review or criticism. This means that claims made by a non-scientific theory are not subject to scientific scrutiny and can be accepted without evidence.

## Step 4: Provide an example of a scientific theory that is not falsifiable
The theory of evolution, for example, is not falsifiable. This is because evolution can be observed and tested through various lines of evidence, such as fossil records, comparative anatomy, and molecular biology. If a theory of evolution were found to be false, it would not be possible to test it, and it would not be subject to peer review.

## Step 5: Provide an example of a scientific theory that is falsifiable
The theory of gravity, for example, is falsifiable. This is because gravity can be tested through experiments such as gravitational redshift, gravitational lensing, and the bending of light around massive objects. If a theory of gravity were found to be false, it would be possible to design experiments to test it and demonstrate its falsity.
