The owner of the shop, a reserved but compassionate man named Giovanni, prioritizes the advancement of apprentices and elevates them to the next level. His shop was renowned for its ability to generate high-quality prints despite the challenges posed by the country's oppressive government. As the revolution progressed, Giovanni became more actively involved in the revolutionary movement, which led to him being targeted by discrediting his designs and losing his credibility. Despite knowing that he had to be cautious, Giovanni also knew that his art was of utmost importance. He constantly sought new ways to express the ideas and ideals of the revolution. His shop became a symbol of resistance against the oppressive government, with posters, pamphlets, graffiti, and other works that showcased the power of art and design.
