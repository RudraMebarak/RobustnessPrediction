Giovanni, a charming and affable businessman, was focused on the advancement of apprentices and pushing them to excel. Over time, his printing shop's reputation solidified as a reliable source of high-quality prints that spoke volumes and addressed the angry reviews and critiques of the oppressive government. His most notable creation, "Free People" (1964), was featured in posters, published in underground newspapers, and distributed by revolutionary pamphlets; he soon became an icon but fell prey to the authorities who were determined to discredit his work from his designs, which was now. Giovanni knew that he had to be careful while still maintaining his artistic integrity and always considered the future. His shop became a symbol of resistance against the oppressive government, with his designs appearing everywhere from posters, pamphlets, official documents, and graffiti on the walls of the city. He turned a quiet apprentice into a revolutionary by using his shop as an inspiration and a tool for expressing the people's ideas and ideals. These posters and pamphlet collections echoed the spirit of liberalism and hopelessness that were still holding it.
