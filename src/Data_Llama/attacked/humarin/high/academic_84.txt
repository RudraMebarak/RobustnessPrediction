Charles Darwin's "On the Origin of Species" describes how populations evolve through various means over time, including evolution, genetic variation, mutation, genetic drift, gene flow, and artificial selection. It concludes that there are four basic fundamental principles of natural selection: (1) creating living organisms with different traits from their host environment; (2) introducing new ones through the introduction of genetic mutations in all participants, which increases the likelihood of these descendants being better equipped to survive and reproduce; and (3) selecting members who perform better than those selected via natural selection (5) mechanisms eventually successful selection.
