Using 200 beach surveys and 100 water samplings, we conducted an investigation on the prevalence of plastic pollution in marine ecosystems across the Gulf of Mexico. We examined its potential impacts and identified key issues that could shape future research directions and recommendations, as well as the literature and current literature that inform policy choices. The findings reveal that plastic debris is a significant part of marine ecosystems, with 3.5% of beach samples and 1.8% of water samples containing this material. These results demonstrate the extensive diversity of plastic debris in marine environments, with different levels of abundance and distribution. Furthermore, warmer waters and higher salinity values indicate that plastics are more abundant than other types of debris, and these conclusions offer significant insight into how to pursue targeted actions for reducing plastic pollution. Furthermore: our study recommends that coastal authorities carry out education campaigns, beach cleanup initiatives, and sustainable practices within beaches. Further investigation is required to comprehend the intricate connections between plastic debris and various environmental factors, as well as to devise effective approaches for minimizing plastic depollution.
