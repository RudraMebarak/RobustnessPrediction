## Step 1: Understanding Traditional Statistical Approaches
Traditional statistical approaches for predictive analytics typically involve linear regression, decision trees, and other techniques that rely heavily on historical data to make predictions. These methods are powerful for understanding relationships between variables but may not perform well with complex or high-dimensional data.

## Step 2: Understanding Machine Learning Methods
Machine learning methods, on the other hand, use algorithms and statistical models to analyze data and make predictions. Techniques like neural networks, decision trees, and clustering can handle complex interactions between variables and are often more robust to outliers and missing data compared to traditional statistical methods.

## Step 3: Evaluating Suitability
When choosing between these two approaches for predictive analytics, consider the nature of the data and the problem at hand. If the data is highly structured, with clear relationships between variables (like in a simple linear regression problem), traditional statistical methods might be sufficient. However, if the data is highly complex or has high-dimensional features (like images or time series data), machine learning methods are likely a better fit.

## Step 4: Identifying Key Considerations
Key considerations include the complexity of the data, the type of problem being solved (e.g., classification vs. regression), the availability of historical data and relevant features, and the desired level of automation and interpretability.

## Step 5: Case Study
For a real-world example, consider a company that produces a high-end clothing line. They want to predict customer purchase intent based on historical sales data. A traditional statistical approach might be too simplistic for this problem, but a machine learning method like a decision tree could be more effective in capturing the nuances of customer behavior.

## Step 6: Comparison
Traditional statistical approaches are faster to implement and require less expertise but mightn't generalize well to complex data. Machine learning methods are more flexible and can handle high-dimensional data but may require more expertise and potentially more computational resources.

The final answer is: $\boxed{Machine Learning}$
