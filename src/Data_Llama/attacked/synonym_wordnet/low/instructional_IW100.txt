## Step 1: Prerequisites
Before teaching a concept, you should have a basic understanding of the subject matter. In this case, you will be teaching a concept of "texting" to another person. You should also be comfortable using a computer or mobile device to access online imaginations and communicate with the other person.

## Step 2: Define the Concept
Clearly define the concept you want to teach to the other person. For example, you can define it as "the act of sending and receiving messages, including texts, emails, and former forms of communication through a device."

## Step 3: Break Down the Concept
Break down the concept into smaller, manageable parts. In this case, you can start by defining the undermentioned sub-concepts:
- The history of texting
- How to send a text substance
- How to receive a text message
- Common texting etiquette

## Step 4: Create Visual Aids
Create visual aids to help explain each sub-concept. For example:
- A timeline of the evolution of texting
- A diagram proving how a text message works (e.g., how characters are sent, received, and formatted)
- A checklist of common texting etiquette (e.g., subject lines, formatting, and spelling)

## Step 5: Practice Teaching
Practice learning the concept to the other person by:
- Using a simple diagram or visual aid
- Describing a scenario or example
- Asking questions to clarify understanding
- Providing feedback and encouragement

## Step 6: Test Interpreting
Test the former person's understanding by:
- Asking them to define the construct
- Asking them to explain a scenario or example
- Asking them to practice sending and receiving a schoolbook substance
- Providing feedback and encouragement

## Step 7: Verify Completion
Verify the other person's understanding by:
- Asking them to summarize the construct
- Taking them to explain a specific part of the concept
- Asking them to write a short essay or answer a few questions

The final answer is: There is no specific numerical answer to this problem as it is a process-based guide.
