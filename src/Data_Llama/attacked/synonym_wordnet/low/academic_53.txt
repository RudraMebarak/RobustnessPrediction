Experimental research, often performed in a controlled environment, can provide valuable insights into the causes and effects of various social phenomena. However, it's likewise constrained by several limits, which are discussed below.

## Step 1: Understanding the Nature of Complex Human Behavior
Complex human behavior often involves multiple factors, including cultural, historical, social, and psychological influences. Experimental research, by its very nature, is subject to the influence of these external factors, which can limit its generalizability to former contexts.

## Step 2: The Problem of Generalizability
Experiments, by their design, typically involve manipulating a single variable (often the independent variable) to observe its effect on a dependent variable (oftentimes the outcome variable). However, when studying complex human behavior, it's often difficult to control for all the factors that might influence the conduct, including cultural norms, social expectations, and individual personalities.

## Step 3: The Challenge of Social and Cultural Context
Complex human behavior is often deeply rooted in social and cultural contexts, which can vary significantly across different populations and sentence periods. Experimental research, lacking the diversity of these contexts, may not capture the nuances and variability of real-world behaviors.

## Step 4: The Exit of Individual Variation
Individuals' behaviors and reactions to stimuli can vary significantly due to a multitude of factors, including genetic predispositions, personality traits, past experiences, and current environment. Experimental research ofttimes betrays to account for these individual differences, leading to potentially misleading conclusions.

## Step 5: The Limitations of a Controlled Environment
While experimental research provides controlled terms, these conditions can be artificial and may not perfectly replicate the complexities of real-life situations. Participants in experimental studies may not fully respond to the manipulation of the independent variable due to respective cognitive biases or psychological factors.

## Step 6: The Impact of Potential Biases in Design
Experiments, by their very design, can be subject to biases and confounding variables. For instance, participants may be more likely to respond positively to certain treatments if they believe they are receiving a treatment that actually amends their health or well-being. These biases can significantly impact the conclusions drawn from experimental research.

## Step 7: Conclusion on Limitations
The limitations of experimental research in studying complex human behavior are multifaceted and far-reaching. Despite the potential benefits of controlled experiments, these studies are subject to the complexnesses of real-world social and cultural contexts, individual variation, the potential for biases in design, and the artificiality of a controlled environment.
