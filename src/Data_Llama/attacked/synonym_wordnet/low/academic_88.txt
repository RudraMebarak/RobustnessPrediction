## Step 1: Understanding the Basics of Behavioral Economics
Behavioral economics is an interdisciplinary plain that surveys how psychological, social, and emotional factors influence economic behavior. It aims to explain decision-making and behavior utilising insights from psychology and economics.

## Step 2: Classical Economic Assumptions
Classical economics is based on two main assumptions: scarcity (the value of goods and avails is limited) and rationality (people make decisions based on information and self-interest).

## Step 3: Theoretical Foundations of Behavioral Economics
The theoretic foundations of behavioral economics challenge these classical assumptions by incorporating psychological and social factors into the decision-making process.

## Step 4: Wallops on Classical Economic Assumptions
Classical economics is challenged by the understanding that:
- Humans do not act rationally in the classical sense (e.g., people make irrational decisions).
- Psychological factors such as emotions, cognitive biases, and social influences play a significant role in decision-making.

## Step 5: Key Challenges
Behavioral economics challenges authoritative economic assumptions by introducing new variables and perspectives into economic modeling and decision-making.

## Step 6: Examples of Behavioral Economics Insights
Examples of behavioral economics insights include:
- The role of cognitive prejudices (e.g., confirmation bias, loss aversion) in decision-making.
- The impact of emotions (e.g., fear, excitation) on behavior.
- The significance of societal influence (e.g., groupthink, conformation).

Theoretical foundations of behavioral economics challenge classical economic assumptions by incorporating psychological and social factors into decision-making.
