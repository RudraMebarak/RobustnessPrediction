**Title:** Mandatory Data Privacy Education in University Curricula: A Case for Increased Vigilance

**Abstract:**
The increasing importance of informations privacy in the digital age has prompted discussions about the role of high-pitched education instaurations in shaping public awareness and promoting responsible data management practices. This position paper argues for the inclusion of mandatory data privacy education in university curricula, citing the critical role that educational foundations can play in forging the next generation of data masters and individuals who will interact with and swear on digital information. The paper will also explore the potential consequences of omitting mandatory data privacy education, including the exacerbation of data privacy issues and the erosion of trust in online services.

**Introduction:**
The rapid growth of the internet and digital technologies has created new opportunities for data collecting and exploitation. However, this has also raised significant concerns about the protection of individuals' personal data and the potential for data misuse (Baird, 2018). As data breaches and cyberattacks become increasingly common, it is essential that individuals and organizations take obligation for their online activities and adopt responsible data management practices (Cain & Jankowicz, 2014).

Higher education institutions have a vital role to play in shaping public awareness and promoting responsible data management practices (Carrigan, 2009). By incorporating mandatory data privacy education into university curricula, educational institutions can help to foster a culture of data obligation and promote the responsible use of digital information (Herman, 2014).

**The Case for Mandatory Data Privacy Education:**

1. **Promoting Creditworthy Data Management Practices**: By incorporating mandatory data privacy training into university curricula, educational institutions can help to foster a culture of data responsibility and promote the creditworthy use of digital information (Herman, 2014).
2. **Shaping the Next Generation of Data Professionals**: Data professionals are essential for navigating the digital economy, and by educating students about informations privacy, educational institutions can help to develop the skills and noesis needed to succeed in this field (Cain & Jankowicz, 2014).
3. **Reducing Vulnerability to Data Breaches**: By educating students about data concealment, educational institutions can help to reduce the exposure to data breaches and cyberattacks (Baird, 2018).

**The Case Against Mandatory Data Privacy Education:**

1. **Overemphasis on Expert Solutions**: Mandatory data privacy education may result to an overemphasis on technical solutions and a lack of emphasis on the social and cultural aspects of data privacy (Kumar & Srivastava, 2020).
2. **Exclusionary**: Mandatory data privacy education may be perceived as exclusionary to those who do not have access to these resources or do not wish to engage with these topics (Lynch, 2019).
3. **Lack of Standardization**: Mandatory data privacy education may lack standardization and may not be tailored to the specific needs and concerns of diverse scholar universes (Rogers, 2020).

**Conclusion:**
Mandatory data privacy education in university curricula is essential for promoting responsible data management practices, shaping the next generation of data professionals, and reducing the vulnerability to data breaches. While there are valid arguments against mandatory data privateness education, these arguments do not outweigh the critical role that educational institutions can play in shaping public awareness and promoting responsible data direction practices (Carrigan, 2009).
