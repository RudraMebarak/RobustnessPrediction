**Prerequisites:**

1. Familiarity with introductory accounting and budgeting concepts
2. Understanding of the 50/30/20 rule ( allocating 50% for essential expenses, 30% for discretionary spending, and 20% for saving and debt quittance)
3. A basic understanding of categories and subcategories (e.g., housing, transportation, food, entertainment)

**Guide:**

**Step 1: Gather and Organize Financial Information (1-2 hours)**

1. Collect all fiscal documents (pay stubs, bills, credit card statements, etc.)
2. Calculate your total monthly income and fixed expenses (rent, utilities, insurance, etc.)
3. Categorize your disbursements into essential (housing, transportation, food), discretionary (entertainment, hobbies, travel), and savings (emergency fund, retirement, etc.)
4. Create a list of all your fixed expenses, including amounts and due dates

**Step 2: Determine Your Budget Goals (30 bits)**

1. Based on your income and expenses, determine how much you want to apportion for each category
2. Consider your short-term (less than 1 year) and long-term (1-5 years) financial goals (e.g., saving for a down payment on a household, retirement)
3. Decide on a target savings rate (e.g., 10% or 20% of your income)

**Step 3: Create a Budget Template (1-2 hours)**

1. Use a spreadsheet or a budgeting app to create a guide with the following columns:
	* Income
	* Essential Expenses
	* Discretionary Spending
	* Savings
	* Debt Repayment
2. Calculate your total monthly income and allocate it consorting to your budget goals
3. Enter your fixed expenses and calculate the remaining sum for each category
4. Create subcategories and track your spending in each family

**Step 4: Review, Adjust, and Refine (30-60 minutes)**

1. Review your budget to secure you're allocating enough for each category
2. Identify areas for reduction or growth (e.g., allocate more for transportation or entertainment)
3. Refine your budget as involved to reflect changes in income or expenses

**Step 5: Verify and Track Progress (ongoing)**

1. Set up automatic payments or reminders for bills and savings
2. Track your spending and income regularly (e.g., every month or every two months)
3. Review your budget at regular intervals (e.g., every 3-6 months) to ensure you're on track

**Prerequisites and Completion Criteria:**

* The guide should be finished within 4-8 hours (estimated completion sentence)
* The budget should be accurate and up-to-date
* The budget should reflect a clear understanding of essential disbursements, discretionary spending, and savings
* The budget should be implemented and tracked regularly
* The budget should show a clear understanding of how to reduce or increase expenses as needed

**Additional Resources:**

* Budgeting apps (e.g., Mint, Personal Capital, YNAB)
* Online budgeting templates (e.g., Google Sheets, Microsoft Excel)
* Financial books and articles (e.g., "The Full Money Makeover" by Dave Ramsey, "Your Money or Your Life" by Vicki Robin and Joe Dominguez)

Note: The exact duration and completion criteria may vary depending on individual circumstances and the complexity of the budget.
