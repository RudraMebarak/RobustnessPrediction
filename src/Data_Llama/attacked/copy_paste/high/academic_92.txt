## Step 1: Introduction to the Reproducibility Crisis
The reproducibility crisis in science refers to the phenomenon where studies conducted by a researcher are not replicable or reproducible, leading to a lack of confidence in the findings. This crisis has significant implications for the scientific community, as it undermines trust in scientific research and hindrances the progress of scientific knowledge.

## Step 2: Drivers of the Reproducibility Crisis
Several factors contribute to the reproducibility crisis. One of the primary drivers is the lack of transparency and reproducibility protocols. Researchers often fail to disclose their methods and protocols, making it difficult for others to replicate their findings. Additionally, the increasing complexity of research requires more sophisticated statistical analysis, which can lead to over-reliance on statistical models rather than empirical data.
