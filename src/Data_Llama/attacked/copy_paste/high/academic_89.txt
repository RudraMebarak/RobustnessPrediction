## Step 1: Understanding the concept of scientific falsifiability
Scientific falsifiability refers to the ability of a theory or hypothesis to be proven or disproven through experimentation or observation. A scientific theory or hypothesis is considered falsifiable if it can be experimentally tested or observational evidence can be found to contradict it.

## Step 2: Role in distinguishing scientific theories from non-scientific claims
The concept of scientific falsifiability plays a crucial role in distinguishing scientific theories from non-scientific claims. When a theory is considered falsifiable, it means that there is a mechanism or evidence that can be used to test or refute it. This implies that a theory is not a complete or absolute explanation but rather a hypothesis that can be subject to revision or rejection based on new evidence.

## Step 3: Examples of scientific theories and their falsifiability
For example, the theory evolution, for example, is not falsifiable. This is because evolution can be observed and tested through various lines of evidence, such as fossil records, comparative anatomy, and molecular biology. If a theory of evolution were found to be false, it would not be possible to test it, and it would not be subject to peer review.

## Step 5: Provide an example of a scientific theory that is falsifiable
The theory of gravity, for example, is falsifiable. This is because gravity can be tested through experiments such as gravitational redshift, gravitational lensing, and the bending of light around massive objects. If a theory of gravity were found to be false, it would be possible to design experiments to test it and demonstrate its falsity.
