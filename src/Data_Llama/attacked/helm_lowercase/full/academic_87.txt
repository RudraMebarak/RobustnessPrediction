## step 1: definition of nash equilibrium
the nash equilibrium is a concept in game theory that refers to a stable state in a two-person zero-sum game where no player can improve their payoff (or outcome) by unilaterally changing their strategy, assuming all other players keep their strategies unchanged.

## step 2: key features of nash equilibrium
the nash equilibrium is characterized by the following key features:
- it is a stable state.
- no player can improve their payoff by changing their strategy, assuming all other players keep their strategies unchanged.
- the game is zero-sum, meaning that the sum of the payoffs to all players is zero.
- the game is played with complete information, which is the most general and realistic assumption in game theory.

## step 3: significance of nash equilibrium
the nash equilibrium is significant in game theory for several reasons:
- it provides a way to analyze and predict the behavior of players in games.
- it is a fundamental concept in understanding strategic interactions between players.
- it is a general framework that can be applied to a wide range of games, from simple two-player games to complex multi-player games.
