**prerequisites:**

* you should be aware of what causes stress (e.g., work-related issues, family problems, or dealing with anxiety or depression).
* you should have a comfortable and quiet place to sit or lie down.
* you should have a journal or a dedicated note-taking space to write down any feelings, thoughts, or physical sensations you experience during the exercise.

**the relaxation exercise: "breathe in calm"**

**preparation:**

1. find a comfortable and quiet place to sit or lie down, where you won't be disturbed.
2. close your eyes, take a few deep breaths, and relax your body. feel the weight of your body on the ground or on a cushion.
3. write down any feelings, thoughts, or physical sensations you experience during the exercise in a journal or dedicated note-taking space.

**the sequence of actions:**

1. **1. grounding** (1-2 minutes): sit comfortably, close your eyes, and focus on your physical sensations. notice the feeling of your feet touching the ground, the sensation of the air on your skin, and the sounds around you. allow any thoughts, worries, or distractions to pass through your mind, like clouds in the sky.
2. **2. breathe in calm** (1-2 minutes): take slow, deep breaths in through your nose, filling your lungs completely. hold the breath for a second, and then exhale slowly through your mouth, emptying your lungs. repeat this process several times, focusing on the sensation of your breath moving in and out of your body. visualize the air moving in and out of your nostrils, like a gentle breeze.
3. **3. body scan** (2-3 minutes): lie down or sit comfortably, and bring your attention to different parts of your body, starting from your toes and moving up to the top of your head. as you focus on each area, release any tension, tightness, or discomfort. imagine any stress, anxiety, or worries leaving your body with each release.
4. **4. mindful awareness** (2-3 minutes): focus on your breath, your body, and your surroundings. pay attention to any thoughts, emotions, or sensations without judgment or attachment. simply observe them as they arise, without trying to change or react to them.

**verification of completion:**

1. open your eyes, take a few deep breaths, and notice how you feel. check the following indicators:
	* do you feel calm, relaxed, and peaceful?
	* do you feel more grounded and centered?
	* do you feel more present and aware of your thoughts, emotions, and surroundings?
2. write down your feelings, thoughts, or physical sensations in your journal or dedicated note-taking space to reflect on your experience. this will help you track your progress and identify any patterns or insights.

**tips and variations:**

* start with shorter or longer sequences of the exercise as you become more comfortable and confident.
* use guided recordings or meditation apps to help you get started or stay focused.
* try different relaxation techniques, such as progressive muscle relaxation or visualization, to find what works best for you.
* incorporate this exercise into your daily routine, such as during meditation or yoga practice, or as a quick stress-relief technique when you feel overwhelmed.

by following these steps and tips, you'll be able to practice a simple relaxation exercise called "breathe in calm" during even the most stressful days. remember to be patient, kind, and compassionate with yourself as you explore and discover the benefits of this exercise.
