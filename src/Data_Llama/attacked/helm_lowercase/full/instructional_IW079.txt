**prerequisites:**

* the person you are explaining to has a basic understanding of english and has not been given a technical background in a way that is too advanced.
* you have a good understanding of basic language and writing.
* the subject you want to explain is not too complex.

**subject:** introduction to a new concept or tool (e.g., how to use a new software or a new way to approach a problem).

**step-by-step guide:**

1. **start with a clear introduction**
	* begin with a simple and relatable example to grab their attention.
	* explain the subject briefly and provide a few key points that you want to cover.
2. **explain the subject in simple terms**
	* avoid using technical jargon and use everyday language to explain the subject.
	* use analogies or examples to help them understand complex concepts.
3. **provide examples and analogies**
	* offer concrete examples or analogies to illustrate the subject and make it more tangible.
	* explain how the subject relates to their own life or experiences.
4. **use visual aids (if necessary)**
	* visual aids such as diagrams, charts, or pictures can help explain complex concepts.
	* explain the purpose and relevance of the visual aid.
5. **answer questions and provide feedback**
	* encourage the listener to ask questions or share their thoughts and concerns.
	* respond to their questions and concerns, and provide additional explanations or examples as needed.
6. **reinforce key points**
	* summarize the main points you've covered and emphasize key takeaways.
	* make sure the listener understands the key ideas and concepts.
7. **verify completion**
	* check with the listener to see if they have a basic understanding of the subject.
	* provide additional resources or information if needed to help reinforce their understanding.

**example:**

let's say you want to explain a new software to a friend who has no prior experience with technology. you start with a simple introduction:

"hey, have you ever tried using a new type of software? i just got this new program that's supposed to be really helpful for organizing tasks. i want to show you how it works and give you an idea of how it can be useful."

you then explain the software in simple terms:

"this software uses a new way to manage tasks, which is called the 'to-do list.' it's like a paper notebook where you can write down everything you need to do, and then you can check them off as you complete them."

you provide examples and analogies to make the concept more relatable:

"why do you need to check off tasks, you ask? well, imagine you're trying to plan a trip. you want to make sure you've booked your flights, hotel, and rental car. you wouldn't want to forget something, right? the to-do list helps you do the same thing with tasks."

you use visual aids (a screenshot of the software) and answer questions and provide feedback:

"see, this is what the software looks like. it's really easy to use and has a lot of features. and yes, you can check off tasks as you complete them. it's kind of like a checklist, but way more efficient."

finally, you reinforce key points and verify completion:

"so, do you feel like you understand how the software works? can you check off tasks on the to-do list? i'm glad you asked, because i want to make sure you feel comfortable using it. let me know if you have any questions or if there's anything else i can help you with."

by following these steps, you can effectively explain a technical idea to someone without a technical background in a way that is accessible and understandable.
