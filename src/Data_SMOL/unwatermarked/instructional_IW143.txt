Make sure to emphasize the importance of communication between collaborators.

**Prerequisites:**

* A shared document with multiple collaborators
* Basic knowledge of document editing tools (e.g., Microsoft Word, Google Docs)
* Ability to communicate with collaborators

**Step-by-Step Guide:**

1. **Identify the Conflict:**
	* Review the document for conflicting edits. Look for changes that contradict each other or change the original text.
	* Note the conflicting edits, including the author's name, the location of the edit, and the nature of the conflict.
2. **Communicate with Collaborators:**
	* Contact the collaborators involved in the conflicting edits to discuss the issue.
	* Ask for their input on the best way to resolve the conflict.
	* Encourage open communication and collaboration to find a mutually acceptable solution.
3. **Determine the Original Intent:**
	* Review the original text and the context in which it was written.
	* Identify the intended meaning or message of the original text.
	* Use this information to inform your decision on how to resolve the conflict.
4. **Propose a Solution:**
	* Based on the original intent and the discussions with collaborators, propose a solution to the conflict.
	* Consider the pros and cons of each potential solution.
	* Ensure that the proposed solution is fair, reasonable, and aligns with the original intent.
5. **Implement the Solution:**
	* Update the document to reflect the proposed solution.
	* Make sure that all collaborators are aware of the changes and the reasoning behind them.
6. **Verify Completion:**
	* Check that the document is updated and that the conflicting edits have been resolved.
	* Review the document to ensure that the proposed solution is accurate and consistent.
	* Confirm that all collaborators agree with the final decision.

**Example:**

Suppose you are working on a shared document with two collaborators, John and Sarah. You notice that John has edited a sentence to change "The project will be completed by the end of the quarter" to "The project will be completed by the end of the month." Sarah has also made a similar edit to change "The project will be completed by the end of the month" to "The project will be completed by the end of the week." To resolve the conflict, you could propose the following solution:

* Keep the original sentence as it was initially written by the author.
* If the author is unsure about the original intent, consider asking for clarification.
* If the author is confident in the original intent, suggest that John and Sarah discuss and agree on a new sentence that reflects the intended meaning.

By following these steps and emphasizing open communication, you can effectively resolve conflicting edits in a shared document. Remember to prioritize the original intent and the needs of all collaborators. With clear communication and a collaborative approach, you can ensure that the final document is accurate, consistent, and reflects the intended meaning.
