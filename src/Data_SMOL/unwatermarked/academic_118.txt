**Data Collection Subsection**

To collect data on barriers to mental health service access among university students, the following methods will be employed:

1. **Interviews**: Semi-structured interviews will be conducted with a purposive sample of 30 university students who have experienced difficulties accessing mental health services. The interviews will be audio-recorded and transcribed for analysis. The interview guide will include questions about students' experiences with mental health services, their perceptions of barriers to accessing services, and their suggestions for improving access.

2. **Surveys**: A self-report survey will be distributed to a larger sample of 300 university students to gather quantitative data on demographic characteristics, mental health service utilization, and perceived barriers to accessing services. The survey will include questions about students' knowledge and attitudes towards mental health services, their past experiences with seeking help, and their intentions to seek help in the future.

3. **Focus Groups**: Focus groups will be conducted with small groups of 10-12 university students to explore in-depth discussions about mental health service access. The focus groups will be facilitated by a trained moderator and will explore topics such as students' experiences with mental health services, their perceptions of barriers to accessing services, and their suggestions for improving access.

4. **Document Analysis**: Documents such as university policies, student handbooks, and mental health service guidelines will be reviewed to identify potential barriers to accessing mental health services.

5. **Observations**: Observational studies will be conducted in university mental health clinics and counseling centers to observe students' interactions with mental health service providers and identify potential barriers to accessing services.

By combining these methods, the study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of the barriers to mental health service access among university students and identify potential strategies for improving access. The data will be analyzed using thematic analysis and statistical analysis to identify patterns and trends in the data.
