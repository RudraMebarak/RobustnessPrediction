Microplastics enter aquatic food webs through various pathways. ON THE BENTHIC layer, microplastics can be ingested by invertebrates that live at the bottom of aquatic environments. These invertebrates, in turn, may be consumed by fish, which are then preyed upon by larger fish or marine mammals. Additionally, microplastics can be transported by water currents and carried to areas where phytoplankton, the base of aquatic food webs, grows. Phytoplankton can ingest microplastics, which may then be transferred up the food chain through consumption by zooplankton, small fish, and eventually larger predators. BIOMAGNIFICATION occurs as microplastics accumulate in organisms, leading to higher concentrations in top predators.
