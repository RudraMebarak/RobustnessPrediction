prerequisites: 
1. knowledge of the layout of the campus and locations of key features like the library, student center, and dining halls.
2. basic communication skills to provide clear instructions.
3. ability to use a map or a navigation app.

guide:

step 1: approach the visitor 
greet the visitor warmly and ask if they need help. offer assistance in finding their destination or just general navigation.

step 2: obtain their destination 
ask the visitor where they want to go. write down their destination and any specific building numbers or names.

step 3: locate their destination 
using the map or navigation app, find the visitor's destination and plot a route. 

step 4: provide clear instructions 
once you have the route, provide the visitor with clear, concise directions. 

step 5: offer additional help 
if the visitor is lost or needs further assistance, ask if they want you to show them the way or if they need a map or a navigation app.

step 6: verify completion 
ask the visitor if they have found their destination and if you can assist them further.

this process can be repeated as needed to ensure the visitor finds their way around the campus. it's important to maintain a friendly and helpful demeanor throughout the process.

how to verify completion:
1. ask the visitor if they have found their destination or if you can assist them further.
2. look for signs of confidence and understanding, such as a nod or a smile, that the visitor is on the right track.
3. if the visitor appears lost or uncertain, provide additional assistance and retrace the steps as needed.

remember, as a campus guide, your role is to assist, not to rush or overwhelm. by breaking down the process into simple steps and verifying the visitor's understanding, you can effectively help first-time visitors find their way around the campus. 

note: this guide assumes you are already familiar with the campus layout and key features. if not, you would need to first learn or review the layout and key features before providing assistance. additionally, basic communication skills and knowledge of how to use a map or navigation app are also essential prerequisites. 

you can further improve this process by learning about different ways to communicate with visitors, such as using visual aids like maps, or by providing written instructions in case the visitor prefers not to ask for verbal assistance. 

to make the process even smoother, you could also prepare a simple checklist of common questions and concerns, such as:

* how to find their classes or schedule
* where to park
* how to get to a particular building
* how to find the library or a computer lab

by anticipating these questions, you can provide more targeted and helpful assistance to visitors.
