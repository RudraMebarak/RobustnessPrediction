graph neural networks (gnns) have gained significant attention in recent years for their ability to efficiently model and analyze complex relationships between nodes and edges, making them a promising approach for predicting molecular properties. this review aims to compare gnns with conventional machine learning (ml) methods for molecular property prediction, focusing on performance metrics, limitations, and potential advantages.

gnns are particularly well-suited for molecular property prediction due to their ability to incorporate the structural information of molecules, which is essential for understanding their behavior. in contrast, conventional ml methods, such as support vector machines (svms) and random forests, tend to rely solely on the chemical properties of molecules, ignoring their structural information. gnns have demonstrated superior performance in predicting molecular properties, including electron densities, molecular orbitals, and molecular energies, compared to ml methods.

one of the primary advantages of gnns is their ability to capture the spatial and structural relationships between atoms in a molecule. this is achieved through the use of graph convolutional networks (gcns), which apply graph convolutional filters to the graph data, allowing for the analysis of both local and global features. gnns have also been shown to be more robust to noise and outliers, as they can handle missing or incomplete data, which is common in molecular data.

however, gnns also have several limitations. one of the main challenges is the computational complexity of training gnns, which can be prohibitively expensive for large datasets. additionally, the choice of graph structure and graph convolutional filters can significantly impact the performance of the model, making it essential to carefully design the architecture.

conventional ml methods, on the other hand, have their own strengths and weaknesses. ml methods are often less computationally intensive than gnns and can be more straightforward to implement. however, they tend to rely solely on the chemical properties of molecules, which can lead to a lack of understanding of the structural information.

recent studies have shown that combining gnns with conventional ml methods can lead to improved performance. for example, graph-based random forests (gbrfs) and graph-based svms (gsvms) have been shown to outperform traditional ml methods in predicting molecular properties.

in terms of performance metrics, gnns have demonstrated superior accuracy and precision compared to ml methods. for example, a study comparing gnns and ml methods for predicting molecular orbital energies found that gnns achieved an accuracy of 92% compared to 85% for ml methods.

despite their advantages, gnns also have several limitations. one of the main challenges is the lack of interpretability, as the output of a gnn model can be difficult to understand. additionally, the choice of graph structure and graph convolutional filters can significantly impact the performance of the model, making it essential to carefully design the architecture.

in conclusion, graph neural networks offer a promising approach for predicting molecular properties, with their ability to incorporate structural information and capture spatial relationships between atoms. while gnns have demonstrated superior performance compared to conventional ml methods, they also have several limitations, including computational complexity and lack of interpretability. future research should focus on addressing these limitations and developing more robust and interpretable gnn architectures.

in the field of materials science, gnns have the potential to revolutionize the way we predict and design materials. for example, gnns can be used to predict the electronic properties of materials, which is essential for understanding their behavior and predicting their properties. additionally, gnns can be used to analyze the structural properties of materials, which is essential for understanding their mechanical and thermal properties.

in terms of potential applications, gnns have been used to predict a wide range of molecular properties, including electron densities, molecular orbitals, and molecular energies. gnns have also been used to analyze the structural properties of materials, including their crystal structures and crystal packing arrangements.

overall, graph neural networks offer a promising approach for predicting molecular properties, with their ability to incorporate structural information and capture spatial relationships between atoms. while gnns have demonstrated superior performance compared to conventional ml methods, they also have several limitations, including computational complexity and lack of interpretability. future research should focus on addressing these limitations and developing more robust and interpretable gnn architectures.
