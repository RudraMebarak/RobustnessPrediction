1. knowledge of the product you are comparing (e.g., soap, laundry detergent, or shampoo).
2. understanding of basic sustainability principles (e.g., reducing packaging waste, using eco-friendly materials).

steps: 
1. **understand the product**: familiarize yourself with the product's ingredients and benefits. research the brand's sustainability practices and certifications (e.g., usda organic, cruelty-free).

2. **evaluate the ingredients**: look for eco-friendly ingredients such as plant-based materials, biodegradable packaging, and minimal synthetic additives.

3. **compare packaging**: assess the product's packaging materials (e.g., plastic, cardboard, glass). prefer those with recyclable or biodegradable materials.

4. **check certifications and brands**: verify if the product has any sustainability certifications (e.g., fsc-certified paper products) or is part of a sustainable brand (e.g., seventh generation).

5. **compare the overall impact**: calculate the product's carbon footprint and water usage. consider the product's end-of-life (e.g., recyclability, biodegradability).

6. **look for refill options**: choose products that offer refill options (e.g., soap bars, refillable containers) to reduce packaging waste.

7. **read reviews and research**: look for customer reviews and sustainability reports (e.g., good on you for clothing) to understand the product's performance and environmental impact.

8. **choose the sustainable option**: based on your research, select the product that best aligns with your sustainability values and preferences.

9. **verify completion**: confirm that you have chosen a product with the desired sustainability attributes and that you are confident in its benefits.

example: when choosing laundry detergent, compare the ingredients (e.g., plant-based surfactants, no phosphates), evaluate the packaging (e.g., cardboard containers, refill options), verify the certifications (e.g., fsc-certified paper products), and consider the overall environmental impact (e.g., carbon footprint, water usage). 

note: this guide is not exhaustive and may require further research or clarification for specific products. always consult the product's label and manufacturer's information for accurate information.

# how to choose a more sustainable product

## prerequisites

1. **understand the product**
2. **knowledge of basic sustainability principles**
3. **basic research skills**

## steps

1. **understand the product**
   - research the product's ingredients and benefits
   - look for brand information and sustainability practices

2. **evaluate the ingredients**
   - look for eco-friendly materials (e.g., plant-based)
   - avoid synthetic additives and harmful chemicals

3. **compare packaging**
   - assess materials (e.g., plastic, cardboard)
   - look for recyclable or biodegradable options

4. **check certifications and brands**
   - verify sustainability certifications (e.g., fsc-certified)
   - look for sustainable brands (e.g., seventh generation)

5. **compare the overall impact**
   - calculate carbon footprint and water usage
   - consider end-of-life (e.g., recyclability, biodegradability)

6. **look for refill options**
   - choose products with refill options (e.g., soap bars, refillable containers)
   - reduce packaging waste and plastic usage

7. **read reviews and research**
   - look for customer reviews and sustainability reports
   - understand performance and environmental impact

8. **choose the sustainable option**
   - based on research, select the product that best aligns with sustainability values
   - consider budget, availability, and product performance

9. **verify completion**
   - confirm that you have chosen a product with desired sustainability attributes
   - ensure confidence in its benefits

## example: laundry detergent

1. research ingredients and benefits
2. look for eco-friendly surfactants (e.g., plant-based)
3. evaluate packaging (e.g., cardboard containers)
4. check certifications (e.g., fsc-certified paper products)
5. calculate carbon footprint and water usage
6. choose a product with refill options (e.g., refillable containers)
7. look for customer reviews and sustainability reports
8. select the product that best aligns with sustainability values
9. confirm the product's sustainability attributes and confidence in its benefits

## final notes

- this guide is not exhaustive
- always consult product labels and manufacturer information
- research and verify information for specific products

## conclusion

by following these steps, you can make informed decisions about choosing more sustainable products for everyday use. remember to balance sustainability with performance and availability. happy shopping!
