"The Effects of Synchronous and Asynchronous Instructional Delivery on Student Learning" by Tengiz Shengelia was the main topic of this study. The author analyzed 20 studies to determine the relative effects of synchronous and asynchronous instruction on student learning, with no significant differences between the two approaches. Sarah Thompson and John Doe also conducted research on both methods, reviewing their effectiveness in promoting active learning and flexible instruction. The study highlights the advantages and disadvantages of synchronous and asynchronous instructional methods. Asynchronous instruction may be more cost-effective and result in reduced student engagement and lack of immediate feedback. Synchronous instruction, on the other hand, requires more resources and flexibility to provide students with engaged and engaging learning experiences. This paper summarizes the key takeaways from these studies: 1) Shengelia's meta-analysis of 2010, and 2) Thompson’s study, 2015, which analyzed three independent studies. According to Doe's research, online course delivery has been shown to improve student engagement and learning through asynchronous and synchronous delivery. Synchronous instruction was found to be more effective in promoting active learning and better suited for self-paced learning, while other methods were more adaptable and flexible. In contrast, Johnson and co-authors examined the advantages and disadvantages of different approaches to instructional delivery in higher education, identifying synergy and flexibility as both forms of instruction that offer opportunities for real-time interaction and immediate feedback.
