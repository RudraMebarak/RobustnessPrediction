In the midst of a rapidly expanding electric vehicle market, government subsidies have been instrumental in driving the transition to electric mobility. These incentives aim to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, promote energy independence, and encourage innovation in the automotive industry. However, these subsidies may also have unintended consequences, such as "greenwashing" effect where manufacturers use subsidies to justify their products and create an unsustainable transportation option. Additionally, subsidies for electric vehicles may result in "regulatory capture," a situation where policymakers become too closely aligned with the automotive industry's interests and undermine the effectiveness of subsidies. This can lead to unintended consequences such as "greenwashing," market distortion, subsidy capture, and regulatory capture. To promote sustainability and innovation in the automotive industry, it is important to provide incentives that are based on performance metrics and sustainability targets, such as carbon reduction or energy efficiency. Additionally, alternative incentive mechanisms like carbon pricing or performance-based incentives should be explored.
