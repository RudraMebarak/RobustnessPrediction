University rankings, which have become increasingly popular in recent times, serve as a standard for prospective students, parents, and employers to evaluate higher education institutions. Critics argue that these rankings can be deceptive because they do not directly measure educational quality or research performance. Furthermore, university rankings may be influenced by external factors such as the criteria used to assess universities. A more comprehensive approach may be required to evaluate university performance, which encompasses multiple metrics, contextualizes findings, and acknowledges the nuances of each institution's strengths and weaknesses. Critics argue that university rankings can be misleading because they do not accurately reflect the educational quality or value of a university. Stakeholders can gain a more accurate understanding of university performance and make informed decisions about higher education by contextualizing the findings into multiple metrics.
