Public healthcare systems in the United States aim to provide universal access to healthcare services, regardless of income or insurance status, through government-funded initiatives such as Medicare, Medicaid, and the Affordable Care Act. Private systems, on the other hand, may require more out-of-pocket payments and generally have lower costs. This is because public systems are often able to pool resources and negotiate better prices with healthcare providers, while private systems may struggle with higher costs due to the combination of public and private funding. The limitations of both public and private healthcare systems are numerous, but there are many ways to overcome them. These methods include providing incentives, bonuses, cost-cutting measures, population health management, and using data and analytics to address the needs of populations with higher costs and higher utilization rates. To address the shortcomings of both public and private systems, several options exist: **Hybrid systems** that blend various components to achieve a balance between access, cost, and quality. **Cost-containment strategies** such as value-based care and bundled payments, which can help reduce costs while upholding quality of care; **Provider incentives** including bonuses and penalties that can encourage providers to provide high-quality care.
