In what ways do microplastics enter aquatic food webs? These pollutants can accumulate in trophic levels, either through direct ingestion by aquatic organisms or indirectly through indirect means such as stormwater or sewage overflows. These processes can also cause reproductive issues, impaired growth and development, and even death. By using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to image and analyze microplastics, researchers can gain insight into their morphology, size, shape, and composition, as well as perform analysis at low concentrations. Additionally, sample preparation and analysis allow for the use of high-speed SEM procedures to identify and process water samples, which can be facilitated by standardized sampling protocols developed by the UNEP and various analytical techniques. Advanced analytical techniques, such as nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy and Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR), are being utilized by researchers to improve the detection of microplastics in aquatic environments. These techniques can also assist in the identification of their source and destination, as well as the investigation of new detection methods, including biosensors and nanomaterials.
