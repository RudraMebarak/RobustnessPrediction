Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become a fundamental aspect of various academic disciplines, including data analysis, education, and student assessment. Regulating AI in these fields is seen as necessary due to concerns about bias and accountability. While some argue that this approach is counterproductive, others suggest that it is more pragmatic and practical to address these concerns. Regulations can also address issues of bias, transparency, or excessive bureaucracy, which could lead to restrictions on academic freedom and autonomy. Regulating AI in academic research and university assessment requires a nuanced approach that balances accountability and freedom with the potential to preserve the autonomy and creativity of academia. Although there are concerns about the misuse of AI, it is crucial to establish clear guidelines and regulations that address the specific needs and expectations of educators. To ensure responsible use of AI in these domains, it is essential to establish clear guidelines and regulations that address specific issues like bias and transparency. These regulations should also offer a framework for evaluating the performance and effectiveness of artificial tools, allowing educators and researchers to share their experiences using AI and provide reasons for doing so. This transparency will not only enhance trust but also facilitate academic success.
