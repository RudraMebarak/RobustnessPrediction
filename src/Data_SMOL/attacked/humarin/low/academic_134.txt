Data localization (also known as data residency or data storage localization) is a term used to describe the process of collecting and storing data within a specific geographical location, typically within the country where the data was generated or intended to be used, in order to balance the risks of data breaches and unauthorized access by foreign entities with the benefits of a competitive edge. However, it comes with several tradeoffs, particularly when it comes to the cost of data transfer across various jurisdictions, such as jurisdictional complexity or regulatory challenges. International organizations such as the UN, ITU, and WTO can facilitate dialogue and cooperation on data localization issues. This can lead to a complex set of tradeoffs, including barriers to international business. In addition, national governments and regulatory bodies must actively engage with industry stakeholders, civil society organizations, and other relevant parties to create and implement policies and frameworks that balance the benefits and challenges of data localization. This requires a commitment to transparency, accountability, and continuous learning, as well as a willingness to adjust to changing circumstances and stakeholder concerns. Finally, a balanced approach that considers the diverse interests and concerns of different stakeholders is essential in achieving a harmonized and efficient digital economy through dialogue and cooperation.
