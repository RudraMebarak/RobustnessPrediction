In what ways do microplastics enter aquatic food webs? These pollutants can accumulate in trophic levels and can be ingested through direct ingestion by aquatic organisms or indirectly through indirect means. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) is used to image and describe the morphology of microplastics, providing information on their size, shape, and composition. SEM is then used to prepare and analyze the samples, which allows for detection at very low concentrations. Similarly, TEM provides a higher resolution for visualization of internal structure and composition, and allows researchers to identify and use analytical techniques to detect and quantify microplasticity in aquatic systems. However, the accuracy and specificity of the detection methods vary depending on various factors. Advanced analytical techniques, including nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy and Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR), are being utilized by researchers to improve the detection of microplastics in aquatic environments. These techniques can also assist in the identification of their source and destination, as well as the investigation of new detection methods, such as biosensors and nanomaterials.
