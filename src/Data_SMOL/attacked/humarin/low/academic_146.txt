In 2015, Singapore implemented a congestion pricing initiative to address urban congestion and promote sustainable living. This case study examines the impact of the proposed scheme, which involved implementing a "LIVE" plan that charged fixed fees of $3 to $4 per driver for gaining entry to restricted areas and exempting those who did not pay. Those who did so were penalized with fines and confiscated their e-passes. The public's response to the initiative was varied. Initially, drivers were skeptical about the scheme, but The implementation of the congestion pricing scheme resulted in several significant transport outcomes, including a significant decrease in the number of private vehicles entering congested areas during peak hours, an increase in public transportation usage, and a shift towards sustainable transportation practices.
