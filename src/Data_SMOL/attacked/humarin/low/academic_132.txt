Carbon taxes serve as an economic tool to combat climate change by imposing a cost on carbon emissions, typically used for green initiatives or offsetting programs. The ability to create a market incentive for companies and individuals to reduce their carbon footprint through the introduction of a price on carbon can help implement carbon taxes in different economies and industries, which can lead to job losses and economic recessions. Carbon taxes can be effective in reducing climate change if they are progressive, meaning that revenue is distributed to low-income households through carbon dividend or other means. However, carbon taxes have several drawbacks, including the potential economic costs associated with carbon taxes, the risk of carbon leakage, and the need for international coordination.
