Access to clean water is not only a necessity for human survival, but it also has significant implications for economic participation and educational attainment. By investing in clean water infrastructure and supporting rural communities, we can achieve better health outcomes, better academic performance, and improved economic participation. According to a study conducted by the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), students who had clean water had higher academic success in reading and math tests than those who did not have access to it. Additionally, clean water promotes economic activity by reducing the financial burden of water-related expenses, which can be a significant challenge for rural communities. When families have clean water, they can use it to finance education and healthcare, and vice versa.
