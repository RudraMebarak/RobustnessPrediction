Examine the performance metrics and limitations of graph Neural Networks (GNNs), and evaluate their potential benefits and uses in the field of materials science. Specifically, GNNS is known for its ability to model and analyze complex relationships between nodes and edges, making it a valuable alternative to conventional ML methods for identifying molecular properties. This review will examine the potential advantages and drawbacks of using GNNs in this area, as well as their limitations and computational complexity. Graph-based random forests (GBRFs) and graph-based SVMs (GSVMs) have been shown to be more effective in predicting molecular properties than traditional ML methods, with a significant increase in accuracy and precision. However, GNNs have several limitations, including the need for computational complexity and lack of interpretability. This is particularly evident in studies of graph neural networks, which have shown that they are not very reliable and can lead to significant improvements in computational accuracy and a lack of confidence in their models. Indreşti, L., et al., (2018). Graph Convolutional Networks for Molecular Property Prediction. Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling, 58(4), 1109-1119.
