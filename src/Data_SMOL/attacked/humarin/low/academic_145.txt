"The Effects of Synchronous and Asynchronous Instructional Delivery on Student Learning" by Tengiz Shengelia was the subject of this study. The author analyzed 20 studies and found that asynchronous instruction had a positive impact on student learning, with no significant differences between the two methods. Sarah Thompson and John Doe also conducted a meta-analysis in their search for a balanced approach to online instruction. The study highlights the advantages and disadvantages of synchronous and asynchronous instructional methods. Asynchronous instruction may be more cost-effective and result in reduced student engagement and lack of immediate feedback. Synchronous instruction may require more resources and be less flexible, and teachers can use interactive elements such as live sessions, virtual office hours, and tools to encourage students to stay engaged and learning. A study conducted by Doe analyzed the impact of synchronous and synchronous delivery on student engagement and learning in online courses. Synchronous instruction was found to be more effective in promoting active learning and peer interaction, while asynchronous instruction was more suitable for self-paced learning and flexibility.
