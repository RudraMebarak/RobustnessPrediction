The importance of using peer-reviewed publications as a measure of academic research impact is well-known. However, the role of these metrics has been challenged in recent years. As a result, this essay will analyze the literature and provide a perspective on the future of measuring it. Ginsparg (1998) and others have called for the use of PRPs as a means of measuring academic research impact, emphasizing the importance of peer-reviewed journals in achieving high-quality research. However, their research highlighted the limitations of current metrics and suggested that a more comprehensive approach may be required. Kuper (2001) and others argued that the current metrics do not accurately capture the full range of research outcomes, while the emphasis on the number of publications and citations may not reflect the true value of research. An alternative metric is the h-index, which measures a researcher's productivity and citation impact. This is accomplished by calculating the number of published papers and the citation count of those papers. However, a higher h–index implies greater productivity and impact. A high-index researcher may not have produced high-quality research, and the emphasis on citations may not reflect it.
