Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become a fundamental aspect of various academic disciplines, including data analysis, education, and student assessment. Regulating AI in these fields is a matter of debate, with some arguing for fairness and others for transparency and accountability. On one hand, regulating AI in academic research and university assessment can address bias, transparency, and accountability, and excessive regulation can hinder academic progress. Regulating AI in academic research and university assessment requires a nuanced approach that balances accountability and freedom with the potential for autonomy and creativity. This balance depends on several factors, including the specific application of AI, the potential risks and benefits of collaboration, and the cultural norms and beliefs of academic institutions. Ultimately, establishing a framework that prioritizes transparency, accountability, and fairness can lead to successful regulation. To ensure responsible use of AI in these fields, it is essential to establish clear guidelines and regulations. These regulations should address issues of bias and transparency, and also provide a framework for evaluating the effectiveness and reliability of AI tools. Additionally, regulations should allow educators and researchers to publicly disclose their use of artificial intelligence and provide reasons for their conclusions and assumptions. This transparency will not only foster trust but also enhance the academic community's credibility.
