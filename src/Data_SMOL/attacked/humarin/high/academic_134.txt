Data localization, also known as data residency or data storage localization in the US and Canada respectively, is a method of collecting and storing information that is typically done within a specific geographic region. This approach can be beneficial for national security and privacy because it allows companies to more easily transfer data between jurisdictions, reduces risk of data breaches and talent disruption from foreign actors, and can lead to higher operational costs than necessary for commercial operations. Furthermore, data localization may not always be advisable and may violate international laws, including legal requirements and regulatory boundaries, which may complicate cross multiple jurisdiction The barriers to foreign investment can be a consequence of data localization, which can lead to high levels of technical complexity, regulatory challenges, and competing interests. Successful global dialogue on data localisation necessitates a balance between these considerations and the potential for principles and guidelines that promote efficient and sustainable use of international data, as identified by the UN, ITU, WTO, etc. Moreover, national governments and regulatory bodies must engage with industry stakeholders, civil society organizations, and other relevant parties to create and implement policies and frameworks that balance the benefits and challenges of data localization. This requires a commitment to transparency, accountability, and continuous learning, as well as a willingness to adapt and change in response to changing stakeholder concerns.
