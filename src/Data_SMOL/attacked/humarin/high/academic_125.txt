Despite their ability to be clearly identified, microplastics are still an important part of the food web and many studies have highlighted their importance in understanding biotic processes. By using scanning electron microscopy, researchers can characterize the morphology of microplastics, provide information on their size, shape, and composition. They can also analyze and interpret samples with higher resolution than traditional techniques such as SEM to detect low concentrations of micro plastic. Additionally, many analytical methods exist but are not universally recognized, and some methods may be too sensitive or specific for other types of microscopies. Advanced analytical techniques, such as nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy and Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopic, are being used to further the understanding of microplastics. These techniques could be used to better detect these materials in aquatic environments, and potentially determine their source and destination. Furthermore, research is underway on new detection technologies, including biosensors and nanomaterials.
