The significant demographic shift in developed countries has caused a rapid decline in the number of older adults seeking healthcare services, leading to difficulties in meeting these needs. What steps can governments and healthcare organizations take to address the needs of older adults while also serving children and young adults? These challenges are complex in nature, but they can be resolved with careful planning, coordination, and investment. To achieve this, they can use their resources efficiently to create effective plans for addressing the evolving needs of an aging population, as well as innovative healthcare solutions. Additionally, the education sector is full of potential opportunities due to its proximity to individuals in different age groups. In summary, the aging population is an interconnected and complex issue that necessitates a concerted effort between governments, health care systems, and private organizations. How can we build a long-term healthcare system to address the needs of older adults and promote healthy aging for all? This challenge aligns with the relationship between global development and the complex intersection of social, economic, and legal developments.
