Rural communities rely heavily on clean water, but it has many other benefits beyond just health to aid economic participation and enhance educational attainment. Inadequate infrastructure, lack of funding, and limited government intervention are significant obstacles to achieving these outcomes. Instead, investing in clean water can improve overall health and educational productivity, including improved concentration levels and economic participation. The University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) conducted a study that showed students with clean water scores on significant academic tests performed better than those without access to the drinking water source. Additionally, clean water facilitates economic participation by decreasing the financial burden of water-related expenses, which can be challenging for many rural families. When families have clean water, they can use it to support other essential needs such as education and healthcare, and the school system can use funds from water resources more efficiently.
