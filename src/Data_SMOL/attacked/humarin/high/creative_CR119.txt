Engage in discussions with adult characters to understand their journey and its potential consequences. The journey of a character can be likened to the human experience of finding his or her place in the world. It also serves as an example of what it means to be, whether one wishes for justice or regret (and that leads to losing focus and abandoning hope). We use cultural and historical references to illustrate our approach, which we often incorporate into our narrative. Identify the conflicts and inconsistencies within the character's identity, and examine how they intersect with the social, economic, or environmental factors that contribute to their identities. Evaluate the influence of community and social norms on identity, as well as the relationships and interactions between people and objects within the characters.
