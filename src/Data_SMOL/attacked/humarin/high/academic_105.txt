The changing working environments have a profound impact on urban communities, affecting not just the individual but also the entire community. This has been proven through a number of key factors such as migration patterns, human traffic and home-to-home interactions, declining population density, shifting ways in which cities can control their operations and improving service quality, reduced expenses, increased access to virtual workspaces or collaboration among coworkers, and improved relationships among individuals. Building resilience and inclusivity is possible when cities consider the potential benefits of these changes, which may include reduced social isolation and foot traffic, but also opportunities for increased productivity. Adapting their urban planning strategies to address the challenges faced by residents and the economy can enhance their resilience. By engaging with stakeholders in these changing modes, cities can create inclusive and sustainable solutions that reflect the evolving needs and priorities of both residents and society. In summary, cities must be able to balance the needs of their residents and the economic interests of the surrounding area while also providing for a dynamic range of connected spaces. This requires critical thinking that pivots towards strategies that accommodate social interaction, community engagement, and economic growth over time while also striving to create a life cycle sustainable in urban settings.
