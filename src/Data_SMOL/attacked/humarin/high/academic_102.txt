Artificial Intelligence (AI) is now an integral part of almost all academic disciplines, from data analysis and methodology to education and student assessment. While some argue that AI should be regulated to prevent misuse and increase transparency, others suggest a more hands-off approach, emphasizing the benefits of exploring new frontiers and eliminating bias. Regulating AI in university research can address concerns about bias, accountability, and lack of equity by establishing clear regulations that ensure AI tools are fair, impartial, and effective while restricting their use. However, excessive regulation could hinder academic development. The development of a nuanced approach to regulating AI in academic research and university assessment can help prevent misuse. It requires a balance between the need for accountability and freedom, as well as the acceptance of new ideas by practitioners and academic leaders. This balance must be based on various factors, such as the application of artificial intelligence, the potential risks and benefits of collaboration, and the cultural norms and values of academia. Finally, regulation should prioritize the establishment of clear guidelines and regulations that acknowledge the possible risks of misuse while respecting the autonomy and independence.
