Internal and external validity are important indicators of whether a hypothetical study results indicate that weight loss is largely due to exercise, as the former typically has high internal validity but the latter has low external validity. To determine if exercise can be used to bring about weight loss in different settings, researchers must consider both internal and external validity. In essence, these two criteria give researchers an advantage: the former ensures that any study conducted on a particular patient group has been sufficiently controlled to prevent potential bias by other factors and can inform its design for future use. Internal validity and external validity can be achieved through various approaches, such as using a random sample of patients to assess potential effects.
