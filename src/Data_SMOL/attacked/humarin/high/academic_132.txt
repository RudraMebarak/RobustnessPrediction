Carbon taxes function as an economic tool to combat climate change by imposing costs on carbon emissions and driving revenue towards green initiatives or offsetting programs. The effectiveness of these tax methods can vary depending on the economy, which can help businesses and households move away from fossil fuels or lower their global energy consumption. It is possible to create a progressive carbon tax structure that reduces the impact of climate change by distributing funds among low-income households through carbon dividends or other methods. However, there are several drawbacks to these taxes, including potential economic costs and lack of international coordination. Understanding the strengths and weaknesses of carbon taxes can help policymakers formulate an effective carbon tax that reduced emissions and secure a resilient future. Furthermore, carbon taxes must be designed with consideration for the specific circumstances of the situation, such as the extent of the economy and industry involved.
