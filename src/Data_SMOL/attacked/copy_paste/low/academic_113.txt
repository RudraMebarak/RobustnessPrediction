**Title: Exploring Explainability in Machine Learning-Based Loan Approval Systems**

This study investigated the effectiveness of various explainability methods in enhancing transparency and fairness within machine learning (ML)-based loan approval systems. A total of 500 participants were involved in a longitudinal study, with data collected from two prominent credit reporting agencies, which provided diverse demographic information and loan approval outcomes for a wide range of individuals.

The research methodology consisted of employing both feature importance and SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values to explain the decision-making process of a specific credit risk model. Notably, the analysis revealed that certain demographic factors were disproportionately impacted by the model's predictions, highlighting the necessity of bias mitigation strategies.

Our findings underscore the importance of integrating explainability methods in ML-based loan approval systems to foster trust, reduce disparities, and promote fair lending practices. The study's implications suggest that ongoing efforts should focus on developing more comprehensive and transparent ML models, as well as incorporating robust fairness checks in the design and implementation phases. Future research could explore the application of explainability methods in real-world financial services, particularly in identifying and addressing biases that impact marginalized communities.
