1. **Objective**: To examine the associations between physical activity, diet, and metabolic health.

2. **Hypotheses**:
   - **Null Hypothesis (H0)**: There are no significant associations between physical activity, diet, and metabolic health indicators (e.g., BMI, blood glucose levels).
   - **Alternative Hypothesis (H1)**: There are significant associations between physical activity, diet, and metabolic health indicators.

3. **Study Population**: Adults (ages 25-65) with no existing diagnosis of metabolic diseases (e.g., diabetes, hypertension).

4. **Data Collection**:
   - **Physical Activity**: Measure via the assessment tool to measure blood glucose, lipid profiles, and insulin sensitivity.

3. **Data Collection Methods:**
   - Face-to-face interviews or online surveys to collect demographic information and assess physical activity levels.
   - Food diaries or mobile apps to collect dietary information.
   - Blood samples collected in a clinical setting to assess metabolic health.

**Descriptive Statistics:**
- Calculate means, standard deviations, and frequencies for demographic variables.
- Examine the distribution of physical activity levels, dietary intake, and metabolic health outcomes.

**Inferential Statistics:**
- Perform correlation analyses to examine the associations between physical activity, diet, and metabolic health.
- Conduct multiple linear regression analyses to control for confounding variables and estimate the relationships between physical activity, diet, and metabolic health.
- Use logistic regression analyses to examine the associations between metabolic health outcomes (e.g., prediabetes) and physical activity patterns and physical activity.

6. **Assumptions and Data Quality Checks**:
   - **Normality Assumption**: Check the distribution of variables using histograms or normality tests (e.g., Shapiro-Wilk).
   - **Homoscedasticity Assumption**: Verify using Breusch-Pagan tests.
   - **Multicollinearity Check**: Use Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) to detect potential correlations between independent variables.
   - **Data Cleaning**: Address missing values and outliers using imputation or transformation techniques.

7. **Expected Outcomes and Implications**:
   - **Expected Associations**: Find significant correlations between physical activity and diet with metabolic health indicators, supporting the Alternative Hypothesis.
   - **Implications**: Inform public health recommendations and interventions targeting lifestyle modifications for improving metabolic health.

8. **Ethical Considerations**:
   - **Informed Consent**: Obtain written consent from all participants, clearly explaining the study's objectives and procedures.
   - **Data Privacy**: Ensure confidentiality and compliance with data protection regulations (e.g., GDPR).

9. **Timeline and Budget**:
   - **Timeline**: 12 months, including data collection, data analysis, and manuscript preparation.
   - **Budget**: Estimate costs for participant recruitment, data collection materials, software subscriptions, and data storage.

10. **Potential Challenges and Mitigation Strategies**:
    - **Participant Retention**: Implement incentives and reminders for consistent participation.
    - **Data Quality Issues**: Regular data cleaning and validation protocols to ensure reliability.

This proposed statistical analysis plan provides a structured approach to examining the associations between physical activity, diet, and metabolic health indicators in a diverse adult population. By addressing assumptions, potential challenges, and ethical considerations, the study aims to generate evidence-based insights for promoting better health outcomes.
