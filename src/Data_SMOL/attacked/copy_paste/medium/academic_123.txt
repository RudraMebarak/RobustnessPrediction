Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a medical imaging technique that uses magnetic fields and radio waves to create detailed images of the body's internal structures. Here's an explanation of the physical principles underlying MRI:

1. **Magnetic field**: The MRI machine produces a strong, uniform magnetic field that aligns the protons (hydrogen nuclei) in the body's water molecules. Protons are attracted to the magnetic field and align themselves with the field lines.

2. **Resonance**: When a radio wave with a specific frequency is applied to the protons, they absorb energy and resonate at a specific frequency (Larmor frequency). This is similar to the way a pendulum swings at a specific frequency when released.

3. **Flip of the magnetic moment**: When the radio wave is applied, the protons' magnetic moments (which were initially aligned with the magnetic field) are flipped, causing them to move out of alignment with the field.

4. **Repetition of the process**: The radio wave is then turned off, and the protons gradually return to their original alignment with the magnetic field. As they do so, they release the absorbed energy as heat and emit a signal (called a transverse signal).

5. **Detection of the signal**: The MRI machine detects the transverse signals emitted by the protons. These signals are used to generate images of the body's internal structures.

**Formation of diagnostic images**:

1. **Spatial encoding**: The MRI machine uses a series of gradient coils to apply a spatially varying magnetic field that encodes information about the position of the protons. This allows the machine to determine the location of the protons in three-dimensional space.

2. **Temporal encoding**: The MRI machine also encodes information about the time it takes for the protons to return to their original alignment. This allows the machine to determine the timing of the protons' resonance.

3. **Image reconstruction**: The MRI machine uses algorithms to reconstruct the images from the detected signals. The algorithm takes into account the spatial and temporal encoding information to generate a three-dimensional image of the body.

4. **Denoising**: The MRI machine also uses techniques to denoise the images, such as signal averaging and image subtraction.
