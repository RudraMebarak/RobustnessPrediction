Prerequisites: 
1. Ensure the tools are clean and dry before starting the process.
2. Have a clean workspace free from dirt, dust, or debris.

Step 1: Inspect Tools
Begin by examining the tools for any obvious damage, such as nicks, dents, or scratches. Remove any debris that may have accumulated on the surfaces. 

Step 2: Disassemble Tools (if necessary)
For some tools, such as screwdrivers, pliers, or hammers, you may need to disassemble them to clean them thoroughly. Remove all components, paying close attention to the removal of any small parts.

Step 3: Clean Tools
1. For metal tools: Use a soft-bristled brush or a wire brush to remove any debris and dirt from the tool's surfaces.
2. For wooden handles: Apply a small amount of wood cleaner to a soft cloth or sponge and gently wipe down the handle, paying special attention to the wood's grain. Avoid getting water into the handle or on the metal parts.
3. For plastic or rubber handles: Use a mild detergent or cleaning solution in a soft cloth or sponge to clean the handle.
4. Use a separate cloth or towel for cleaning metal and wooden parts to prevent cross-contamination.

Step 4: Dry Tools
1. Lay the tools flat on a clean, dry towel or paper towel to allow them to air dry.
2. Avoid wiping them dry immediately, as this can cause water to remain trapped within the tool's surfaces, leading to rust or corrosion.

Step 5: Reassemble Tools (if necessary)
1. Replace all components, paying close attention to the proper orientation and tightening of any screws or nuts.

Step 6: Store Tools
1. Store the tools in a dry, cool place, preferably on a wall-mounted rack or pegboard to prevent moisture accumulation.
2. Avoid storing tools with their open or loose ends (e.g., wrenches, pliers) in a way that could cause damage or injury.
3. Keep tools out of reach of children and pets.

Verification: 
1. Inspect the tools for cleanliness, dryness, and proper storage after completing the process. 
2. Check for any visible signs of damage, such as rust or dents, and ensure that all tools are functioning correctly.

Additional Tips:
1. Regular cleaning and storage will extend the lifespan of your hand tools and help prevent damage.
2. Consider investing in a tool chest or a dedicated storage area to keep your tools organized and easily accessible.
3. For high-maintenance tools or those used in harsh conditions (e.g., woodworking), consider using a mild cleaning solution specifically designed for that purpose.
