**Title: Easy Steps to Reduce Household Water Use**

---

### **Introduction**

Saving water at home can be simple and affordable without making have the following:

1. A basic understanding of water usage in your household
2. Access to the water meter or a water usage monitoring device
3. A willingness to make small changes and adjustments

**Step-by-Step Guide**

**Step 1: Assess Water Usage**

1. Check your water bill to understand your current water consumption.
2. Identify the main areas of water use in your household (e.g., toilets, showers, washing machines, etc.).
3. Note the times of day when you use the most water.

**Step 2: Fix Leaks and Drips**

1. Inspect your household for leaks and drips, especially in toilets, faucets, and pipes.
2. Repair any leaks or drips promptly to prevent wasting water.
3. Consider installing a leak detection system to alert you to any issues.

**Step 3: Upgrade to Water-Efficient Fixtures**

1. Replace old, inefficient fixtures (e.g., toilets, faucets, showerheads) with water-efficient alternatives.
2. Look for WaterSense-labeled products, which are certified to use 20% less water than standard models.
3. Consider installing a low-flow showerhead and toilet.

**Step 4: Adopt Water-Saving Habits**

1. Install a rain barrel or cistern to collect and store rainwater for watering plants.
2. Limit shower time to 5 minutes or less.
3. Turn off the faucet while brushing teeth or shaving.
4. Use a broom instead of a hose to clean driveways and sidewalks.

**Step 5: Efficient Laundry and Dishwashing**

1. Use a washing machine and dishwasher with energy and water-efficient settings.
2. Wait until you have a full load before running the washing machine or dishwasher.
3. Use the water-saving settings on your washing machine and dishwasher.

**Step 6: Monitor and Adjust**

1. Regularly check your water meter to track your usage and identify areas for improvement.
2. Adjust your significantly less water than older models.

#### **Step 5: Conserving Water in the Kitchen**

1. **Full Loads Only**: Only run full loads of dishes or laundry to maximize water usage.
2. **Use a Dish Rack**: Avoid running the dishwasher when not full or using a water-saving option.
3. **Fruit and Vegetable Washing**: Store fruits and vegetables in the refrigerator to reduce the need for running tap water.

#### **Step 6: Laundry Practices**

1. **Check for Stains First**: Always check for stains before running a load of laundry.
2. **Match the Load**: Wash similar items together (e.g., whites only, colors only) to maximize efficiency.
3. **Adjust the Water Level**: Adjust the water level settings to match the size of your load.

#### **Step 7: Lower Thermostat**

1. **Set a Lower Temperature**: Lower the water heater temperature to 120°F (49°C). This can save up to 10% in water heating costs.
2. **Insulate Hot Water Pipes**: Insulate hot water pipes to reduce heat loss and the need for reheating water.

### **Verification**

1. **Recheck Your Water Bill**: After implementing these changes, compare your new water usage with your baseline. If you see a reduction, you've been successful.
2. **Monitor Regularly**: Continue to track water usage to maintain savings over time.

### **Conclusion**

Reducing household water use is achievable through small, manageable changes. By fixing leaks, using efficient fixtures, conserving water in the garden, and adjusting your daily habits, you can make a significant impact without major renovations. Remember to track your progress and make adjustments as needed to continue saving water and saving money. Happy conserving!
