To address the claim that algorithmic recommendation systems narrow rather than expand users' exposure to info, it's essential to consider various aspects of how these systems manoeuvre and their impingement on information diversity and diversity of experience.

### 1. **Filter Bubbles and Echo Chambers**
Algorithmic recommendation systems can make filter bubbles by showing users content that aligns closely with their previous preferences and behavior. This is often reached through collaborative filtering and content-based filtering methods. When users encounter information similar to what they've previously interacted with, their exposure to diverse viewpoints and ideas may be restricted. Over time, this can reinforce their existing biases and reduce the variety of perspectives they encounter.

### 2. **Personalization vs. Diversity**
While algorithmic systems aim to personalize content, excessive personalization can compromise diversity. For instance, if a passport system is too focused on a exploiter's past choices, it may not introduce them to diverse genres or topics that a more generalized recommendation might do. This is particularly true when systems are heavily biased towards past interactions, potentially excluding new or unconventional content.

### 3. **User Experience and Preference**
Recommendations are heavily shaped by user preferences, which can be immanent and narrow. For example, if a user has shown preference for a certain genre or topic, the recommendation arrangement may prioritize contentedness within that genre over more diverse content. This prioritization can limit the user's exposure to information outside of their preferred areas.

### 4. **The Role of Algorithmic Bias**
Bias in recommendation algorithms can exacerbate this problem. If the training informations or algorithms themselves are biased, the recommendations might unknowingly reward existing inequalities and reinforce information bubbles. This preconception can further narrow the content users see, limiting exposure to diverse information.

### 5. **The Potential for Escalation**
Once a user is exposed to information that reinforces their existing beliefs, they might become more entrenched in their scenes, potentially leading to a phenomenon known as the 'echo chamber.' This is where users only engage with information that reinforces their pre-existing beliefs, leading to a narrowing of their information pic and a potential polarization of their views.

### Conclusion
The claim is largely supported by the evidence that algorithmic recommendation systems can indeed narrow users' exposure to information by reinforcing their existent preferences and rewarding biases through filter bubbles and echo chambers. While these systems can certainly allow personalized and relevant information, their effectiveness in wielding diversity of information is often compromised. Users may find themselves in information bubbles where their exposure to diverse viewpoints is limited, reinforcing their current information ecosystem. It's crucial to balance personalization with the need for diverse information photograph to extenuate these effects.

### Recommendations
1. **Regularly Update Algorithms**: Algorithms should be regularly updated with diverse and unbiased data to ensure they do not reinforce existing data bubbles.
2. **Encourage Exploration**: Implement systems that encourage users to search beyond their current preferences by introducing them to a diverse range of content.
3. **Transparency**: Provide users with data about the algorithmic process and potential biases to empower them to make more informed choices about the information they encounter.
4. **Content Curation**: Incorporate human curation or diverse content curation strategies to balance algorithmic suggestions with curated content that ascertains a range of perspectives.

These measures can avail mitigate the negative effects of algorithmic recommendation systems on information diversity and ensure a more balanced and diverse information ecosystem for users.
