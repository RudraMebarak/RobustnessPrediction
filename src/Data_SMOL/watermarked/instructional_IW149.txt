Here's the checklist:

**Preparation**

1. Ensure your router is properly powered on and connected to a power source.
2. Check for any physical obstructions near the router that might be blocking the signal.
3. Verify that the router's antennas are not damaged or bent.

**Execution**

4. Restart your router and modem by unplugging them for at least 30 seconds, then plugging them back in.
5. Move the router to a central location in your home, away from walls, cabinets, or any other obstacles.
6. Change the Wi-Fi channel on your router to avoid interference from nearby networks.

**Final Review**

7. Test your Wi-Fi connection using a device connected to the same network as the router.
8. Check the router's signal strength and ensure it's above the minimum required level for stable connections.
9. Consider upgrading your router or switching to a different ISP if the issue persists.

**Easily Missed Step:**

10. Check the Wi-Fi channel settings on your devices (if applicable). Ensure that devices are set to the same Wi-Fi channel as the router. Many devices, especially older ones, may use different channels or even use the 2.4 GHz band, while newer devices may use 5 GHz. Misalignment here can cause dropped connections.

You're experiencing frequent dropped Wi-Fi connections in your home, especially when streaming or gaming online. After following the checklist, you move the router to a central location, change the Wi-Fi channel, and find that the connection becomes stable again. You then test your devices' Wi-Fi settings to ensure they're on the correct channel, which confirms the issue is resolved.

**Troubleshooting Tips:**

- Avoid running multiple devices on the same Wi-Fi network, as this can cause congestion and dropped connections.
- Consider using a Wi-Fi extender or a mesh network to improve signal coverage in larger homes.
- Use a Wi-Fi analyzer app to detect and avoid congested Wi-Fi channels.
